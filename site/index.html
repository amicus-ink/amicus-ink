Wouldn’t it be nice to have someone who knows the relevant methods literatures always at hand to read your drafts?

AMICUS works through a checklist for each method your paper uses and gives suggestions to improve its technical soundness. Each suggestion cites the page of the methods paper it comes from.

Named for the amicus brief: a friend of the paper, you are the judge.

Version 0.1. Fully open source: fork it, change it, build on it.

Run it on a draft  ·  See the checklists: 14 methods plus a universal checklist

What you get

A report with suggestions for how to improve the paper. Each suggestion quotes the passage of your paper it concerns and gives a methods source if you want to dig deeper.

How it works

Dear author,

I read your paper against the methods literature for the two methods it uses, difference-in-differences and panel data. Of the sixteen difference-in-differences checks, eleven are met, two do not apply, and three I would flag. The most important is the estimator: adoption is staggered and the main specification is two-way fixed effects, with no heterogeneity-robust estimate and no argument that the effect is the same across states. I would also measure the unemployment control before adoption, and cluster the standard errors by state. Each flag below gives its sources and pages.

Where the checks come from

Sources are chosen by what scholars cite and, in part, by what they put on their syllabi. In particular, we start with the methods papers that articles in eight general political science and economics journals cite, and that graduate methods courses assign. From these we build a list of the most influential papers for each method. Every step from source list to finished check is described in the repository. Checklist build procedure · Sources.

Easy to run

Attach your paper in any online chatbot you want and write “Run the checklist at https://amicus.ink/AMICUS.md.”

Or, on your own computer, use a coding agent (Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode): install the AMICUS plugin and ask it to run AMICUS on your paper. More details for either route here.
