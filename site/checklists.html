The checklists

The system has checklists for 14 methods plus a universal checklist, with 298 checks in total. Each check is one thing that at least some of the methods literature says that papers should do. Checks include a rule for when it applies and what counts as meeting it.

Anatomy of a check

Here is one example, from the difference-in-differences checklist.

The requirement
DID-EST-01

A staggered-timing DiD must report a heterogeneity-robust estimate or state an ex-ante justification for assuming homogeneous treatment effects.

In plain words

When units start treatment at different times, the paper should use an estimator built for effects that vary across groups and time, or argue in advance that effects are the same everywhere.

When it applies

staggered_timing: Fires only when the paper states that its treated units begin treatment at different times (words the paper uses: staggered adoption, differential/variable treatment timing, rollout, cohorts first treated in different periods). Near-miss that does NOT fire: the paper states all treated units are treated at one common date (single-onset / block design); a two-period two-group design; or an event study built around one shared adoption date. If the paper never describes its own timing as varying across units, the trigger is not fired and the gated check is NA.

What passes

The paper reports a heterogeneity-robust estimate for its staggered design (a group-time / imputation / stacked / not-yet-treated-control estimator), or plain TWFE accompanied by an explicit ex-ante argument that treatment effects are homogeneous across groups and time.

What is flagged

The paper's staggered-timing effect rests on a single-coefficient or dynamic TWFE regression with no heterogeneity-robust estimate reported and no stated justification for a homogeneity restriction.

Severity if flagged: high.

When the paper is silent

The check is marked flag.
