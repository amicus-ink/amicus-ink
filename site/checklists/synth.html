The checklists

Synthetic control

9 checks, each resting on claims from the methods literature. The 10 sources listed at the end are the ones cited as further reading. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

all
The paper estimates a causal effect by building a data-driven counterfactual for one or a few treated units observed before and after an intervention -- it names or adopts a 'synthetic control', 'synthetic control group', penalized or 'generalized synthetic control', a Bayesian structural time-series / CausalImpact counterfactual, or a factor-model / matrix-completion imputation of the treated unit's untreated outcome, and reports the resulting treated-vs-counterfactual gap. Fires on the paper's own description of its estimator. Does NOT fire on a plain two-way fixed-effects / difference-in-differences regression that reports a treatment coefficient without constructing a weighted or imputed counterfactual series for the treated unit, nor on a cross-sectional matching estimator with no pre/post time dimension; when it is unclear whether the paper constructed such a counterfactual, the module is out of scope.
synthetic_control
Fires only when the paper states that it builds its counterfactual as a weighted combination of donor/control units, with weights chosen to reproduce the treated unit's pre-intervention characteristics (it names a 'synthetic control', a 'donor pool', or reports per-unit weights). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a difference-in-differences that uses a simple unweighted average of all controls; a regression that adjusts for control-unit covariates without constructing unit weights. A coder decides fired-vs-not from the paper's own description of how the counterfactual is built, so there is never an NA-vs-FLAG choice for the same paper.

Identification4

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

SYNTH-IDENT-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must state that units themselves exposed to the intervention (or a similar event) or hit by a large outcome shock during the study window were excluded from the donor/control set.

In plain wordsThe paper should state that it removed from its comparison pool any units that received the intervention or a similar one, or had a large one-off shock to the outcome, during the study period, or that it checked and found none.
Why it mattersThe comparison is meant to show what would have happened without the intervention, so units affected by it or by their own large shocks distort that picture.
CheckWhether the paper states any screening of its donor pool or control series to remove units that themselves received the intervention (or a comparable one) or that were hit by a large idiosyncratic shock to the outcome during the study period.
PassThe paper states a donor-pool / control-set restriction that removes units exposed to the intervention or a similar event, or units subject to a large idiosyncratic shock to the outcome during the period (or states it verified none were present).
FlagThe paper builds its counterfactual from a donor pool or control set without stating any such screening.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2014). Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method. American Journal of Political Science, 59(2), 495-510. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12116, pp. 497, 500
SYNTH-IDENT-02severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must address whether the treated unit's intervention could have affected its control units and, where plausible, the direction of the resulting bias.

In plain wordsThe paper should address whether the intervention could have spilled over onto the comparison units, by arguing this is unlikely, reasoning about which way it would push the estimate, or checking results against possibly affected comparison units.
Why it mattersIf the comparison units were also affected by the intervention, the estimated effect is biased.
CheckWhether the paper contains any assessment of interference or spillover from the treated unit onto its control / donor units -- an argument that it is implausible, reasoning about its direction, or a robustness check addressing controls that might be affected.
PassThe paper addresses possible interference / spillover from the treated unit onto its controls: it argues the no-interference assumption holds, reasons about the direction of any spillover bias, or checks robustness to controls that could be affected.
FlagThe paper makes no mention of possible interference or spillover from the treated unit onto its control / donor units.
Further reading

Journal of the American Statistical Association, 105(490), 493-505. https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746, p. 494
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2014). Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method. American Journal of Political Science, 59(2), 495-510. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12116, p. 504
  • Kay H. Brodersen, Fabian Gallusser, Jim Koehler, Nicolas Rémy, & Steven L. Scott. (2015). Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 9(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/14-aoas788, p. 7 (PDF pagination)
SYNTH-IDENT-03severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must address anticipation -- justifying that the outcome did not respond before the formal intervention date or moving the pre/post cutoff back to the first period it could.

In plain wordsThe paper should address whether the outcome began responding before the intervention's official date, either by arguing it did not or by placing the before/after cutoff ahead of the first date the outcome could react.
Why it mattersThe method assumes no response before the official date, so an early response makes the chosen cutoff misleading and distorts the estimate.
CheckWhether the paper addresses the possibility that outcomes responded to the intervention before its formal date -- by arguing anticipation is absent or by placing the last pre-intervention period before the first date the outcome could respond.
PassThe paper argues anticipation is absent, or sets the last pre-intervention period ahead of the first date the outcome could react to the announced intervention.
FlagThe paper adopts the formal implementation date as the pre/post cutoff without addressing whether the outcome could have responded earlier.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2010). Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California’s Tobacco Control Program. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 105(490), 493-505. https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746, p. 494
  • Dmitry Arkhangelsky & Guido W. Imbens. (2024). Causal models for longitudinal and panel data: a survey. Econometrics Journal, 27(3), C1-C61. https://doi.org/10.1093/ectj/utae014, p. 7
SYNTH-IDENT-04severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 7 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

A paper must state and defend the identifying assumption its counterfactual estimator depends on.

In plain wordsThe paper should name the key assumption its comparison relies on, such as parallel trends (treated and comparison units would have moved together without the intervention), and give at least one argument or piece of evidence that it holds.
Why it mattersThe estimated effect is credible only if the assumption behind the comparison holds in the application.
CheckWhether the paper names and defends the identifying assumption underlying its estimator (parallel trends, the factor / low-rank structure with strict exogeneity, or invariance of the shock/relationship under the intervention).
PassThe paper explicitly identifies the assumption its counterfactual requires and gives at least one argument or piece of evidence for its plausibility in the application.
FlagThe paper presents an effect estimate without ever stating the identifying assumption its estimator depends on, or asserts the assumption with no supporting argument.
Further reading

Estimation1

Whether the estimator recovers the target parameter.

SYNTH-EST-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 6 sourcesapplies to: synthetic_controlsilent: flag

A synthetic-control paper must report the donor units and the weights assigned to them.

In plain wordsThe paper should list the comparison units that make up the synthetic control (a weighted blend of untreated units) and the weight each one receives.
Why it mattersShowing which units contribute, and how much, lets readers see that the comparison is a transparent blend of identifiable units that does not extrapolate.
CheckWhether the paper lists the control units that receive positive weight in the synthetic control and their weight magnitudes.
PassThe paper reports the donor units and their assigned weights (e.g., a weights table or a list of the units receiving positive weight and their shares).
FlagThe paper builds a synthetic control but never discloses which donor units enter it or with what weights.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2010). Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California’s Tobacco Control Program. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 105(490), 493-505. https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746, pp. 494, 500
  • Alberto Abadie & Jérémy L’Hour. (2021). A Penalized Synthetic Control Estimator for Disaggregated Data. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 116(536), 1817-1834. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2021.1971535, p. 1817

Inference1

Standard errors, tests, and their validity.

SYNTH-INFER-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 9 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

A paper reporting a constructed-counterfactual effect must base its uncertainty on a procedure appropriate to few treated units and serially dependent outcomes, not on conventional large-sample independent-observation standard errors alone.

In plain wordsThe paper should measure uncertainty with a method suited to few treated units and outcomes correlated over time, such as placebo tests on untreated units or fake dates, permutation p-values, resampling that keeps time patterns, or, with several treated units, standard errors grouped by unit.
Why it mattersConventional standard errors that treat observations as independent understate the uncertainty in this setting.
CheckWhich procedure the paper uses to attach uncertainty or statistical significance to the estimated treated-vs-counterfactual effect.
PassThe paper reports uncertainty through at least one procedure from this list: a placebo/permutation exercise applying the method to donor units, an in-time (pre-dated) placebo, a permutation or conformal p-value, a block or sieve bootstrap or subsampling scheme that preserves within-unit serial correlation, or -- for a design with several treated units and multiple periods -- standard errors clustered by unit.
FlagThe paper's only stated basis for significance is conventional independent-observation or heteroskedasticity-only (Eicker-Huber-White) standard errors that presume a large-sample normal approximation, or it reports an effect with no uncertainty procedure at all.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2010). Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California’s Tobacco Control Program. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 105(490), 493-505. https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746, p. 497
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2011). <b>Synth</b>: An<i>R</i>Package for Synthetic Control Methods in Comparative Case Studies. Journal of Statistical Software, 42(13). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v042.i13, p. 5 (PDF pagination)

Reporting1

What the paper must state for a reader to evaluate it.

SYNTH-REPORT-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: synthetic_controlsilent: flag

A synthetic-control paper should report how the predictor-importance weighting (V) was determined.

In plain wordsThe paper should explain how it set the relative importance of each predictor (the V weights) in building the synthetic control, for example by minimizing pre-intervention prediction error, cross-validation, or a justified custom choice.
Why it mattersAn ad hoc choice can force matching on characteristics that should not be matched and change which units form the comparison.
CheckWhether the paper states how the predictor-importance weighting was set -- a data-driven procedure minimizing pre-intervention prediction error, a training/validation split, or a stated custom choice -- as opposed to leaving it unspecified or defaulting silently to an identity-style weighting.
PassThe paper reports how the predictor-importance weights were chosen (data-driven minimization of pre-period prediction error, cross-validation, or an explicitly justified custom weighting).
FlagThe paper uses covariate predictors to build the synthetic control but never states how their relative importance was weighted.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2011). <b>Synth</b>: An<i>R</i>Package for Synthetic Control Methods in Comparative Case Studies. Journal of Statistical Software, 42(13). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v042.i13, p. 4 (PDF pagination)
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2014). Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method. American Journal of Political Science, 59(2), 495-510. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12116, p. 498
  • Irene Botosaru & Bruno Ferman. (2019). On the role of covariates in the synthetic control method. Econometrics Journal, 22(2), 117-130. https://doi.org/10.1093/ectj/utz001, p. 126

Data2

Sample construction and measurement.

SYNTH-DATA-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: pass

The paper must not build its counterfactual from any predictor or control series that is measured post-intervention or is itself affected by the treatment.

In plain wordsThe paper should build its comparison only from predictors and control series measured before the intervention or argued to be unaffected by it.
Why it mattersA variable that the intervention itself moves biases the estimated effect.
CheckWhether any variable used to construct the counterfactual (a matching predictor or a control / covariate series) is measured after the intervention or is itself plausibly moved by the treatment.
PassEvery matching predictor and control series used to build the counterfactual is measured before the intervention or is explicitly argued to be unaffected by it.
FlagThe paper builds the counterfactual using a predictor or control series that is measured after the intervention or is visibly affected by the treatment.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2011). <b>Synth</b>: An<i>R</i>Package for Synthetic Control Methods in Comparative Case Studies. Journal of Statistical Software, 42(13). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v042.i13, p. 3 (PDF pagination)
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2014). Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method. American Journal of Political Science, 59(2), 495-510. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12116, p. 498
  • Kay H. Brodersen, Fabian Gallusser, Jim Koehler, Nicolas Rémy, & Steven L. Scott. (2015). Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 9(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/14-aoas788, p. 22 (PDF pagination)
SYNTH-DATA-02severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 6 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

A paper must report the number of pre- and post-intervention periods underlying its estimate.

In plain wordsThe paper should report how many time periods come before and after the intervention, as counts or as a dated span at the data's frequency.
Why it mattersReaders need this to judge whether the method was used where it is credible: a long pre-intervention span reduces bias, and enough later periods are needed to capture the effect.
CheckWhether the paper states how many pre-intervention and post-intervention periods (or the date span at its sampling frequency) enter the analysis.
PassThe paper reports the length of both the pre-intervention and post-intervention windows (period counts or an equivalent dated span at the analysis frequency).
FlagThe reader cannot determine from the paper how many pre-intervention or post-intervention periods the estimate is based on.
Further reading
  • Alberto Abadie, Alexis Diamond, & Jens Hainmueller. (2014). Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method. American Journal of Political Science, 59(2), 495-510. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12116, pp. 497, 500

Journal of the American Statistical Association, 105(490), 493-505. https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746, p. 504
