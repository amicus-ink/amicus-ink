The checklists

Multilevel models

10 checks, each resting on claims from the methods literature. The 5 sources listed at the end are the ones cited as further reading. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

panel_tscs
The paper states it observes the same units repeatedly over multiple time periods and models them across time (panel / time-series-cross-sectional / longitudinal). Fires on that self-description. Does NOT fire: a single cross-section; one long time series of a single unit; repeated cross-sections of different individuals the paper does not describe as tracking the same units.
detection_survey
The paper states it analyzes an occupancy or abundance survey in which present individuals or species can go undetected on a given visit (detection/non-detection or repeated-count survey). Fires on that declared design. Does NOT fire: a census or a survey the paper treats as perfectly observed.
bayesian_mcmc
The paper states it estimates the model by Bayesian MCMC / posterior sampling (e.g. Stan, JAGS, BUGS, brms, or a named Gibbs / HMC / NUTS sampler). Fires on the declared estimation engine. Does NOT fire: a frequentist REML/ML fit (e.g. lme4) with no posterior sampling; a Bayesian model the paper says it fit by a non-sampling approximation.
tscs_panel
Fires when the paper states its data are time-series cross-sectional / panel: the same cross-sectional units observed over multiple time periods. Does NOT fire on a single wave of cross-national data, nor on repeated cross-sections drawn from different individuals each period.
multilevel_model
The paper explicitly states it fits a multilevel / hierarchical / mixed-effects / random-effects model, it names that method or reports coefficients (intercepts or slopes) that vary across a named grouping factor as its analytic model. Fires only on that self-description. Does NOT fire: a random intercept mentioned only as a nuisance absorbing term with no group-level interpretation; clustered/robust standard errors shown in a table with no declared multilevel model; a single-level OLS/GLM.
count_binomial_multilevel
The paper states its multilevel/mixed model has a count or binomial-proportion outcome fit with a Poisson, binomial, or negative-binomial family (grouped counts / proportions from trials). Fires on that declared family. Does NOT fire: a binary 0/1 Bernoulli logistic outcome (which cannot be overdispersed); a continuous Gaussian outcome.
mixed_model_prediction
The paper reports predicted, fitted, or forecast outcome values generated from its mixed/multilevel model. Fires when such predicted values are presented. Does NOT fire: reporting only estimated coefficients and variance components with no predicted outcome values.

Identification2

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

MULTILEVEL-IDENT-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: panel_tscssilent: flag

A panel/TSCS model must include a time-period component so that period-common shocks are captured rather than left in the errors.

In plain wordsA model of the same units observed over several time periods should include a component for time periods, such as year indicators, a period-level random effect, or a modeled common time trend.
Why it mattersWithout it, shocks that hit all units in the same period are left in the error term instead of being captured by the model.
CheckWhether the model includes a time-period component, year/period indicators, a period-level random effect, or an explicit modeled time trend, in addition to whatever unit structure it carries.
PassThe model includes an explicit time-period component (period indicators, a period-level random effect, or a modeled common time trend) capturing period-common variation.
FlagThe panel/TSCS model contains no time-period component, leaving year-common shocks unmodeled in the error term.
Further reading
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, p. 244
  • Boris Shor, Joseph Bafumi, Luke Keele, & David S. Park. (2007). A Bayesian Multilevel Modeling Approach to Time-Series Cross-Sectional Data. Political Analysis, 15(2), 165-181. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpm006, p. 166
MULTILEVEL-IDENT-02severity if flagged: highlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: detection_surveysilent: flag

An occupancy/abundance survey with imperfect detection must model a detection process separate from the state process and use replicate visits to identify it.

In plain wordsA survey of whether a species is present, or how many individuals there are, should model the chance of detecting it separately from whether it is actually there, and should visit each site more than once.
Why it mattersSomething present can be missed on a visit, and only repeat visits let the model tell missed detections apart from true absence.
CheckWhether the model specifies a detection (observation) process distinct from the occupancy/abundance (state) process, and whether the design provides replicate visits per site to identify it.
PassThe model includes an explicit detection-probability parameter for the observation process separate from the occupancy/abundance state, and the design records more than one replicate visit per site.
FlagThe survey treats non-detections as true absences (no detection parameter), or reports a single visit per site so detection cannot be separated from occupancy.
Further reading
  • Ian Fiske & Richard B. Chandler. (2011). <b>unmarked</b>: An<i>R</i>Package for Fitting Hierarchical Models of Wildlife Occurrence and Abundance. Journal of Statistical Software, 43(10). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v043.i10, pp. 1, 4 (PDF pagination)

Inference3

Standard errors, tests, and their validity.

MULTILEVEL-INFER-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: bayesian_mcmcsilent: flag

A Bayesian MCMC multilevel fit must report a convergence diagnostic with Rhat at or below ~1.1 for all parameters.

In plain wordsA Bayesian model fit by simulation (MCMC) should report a convergence diagnostic called Rhat for all parameters and show that it is at or below about 1.1; values above 1.05 are borderline.
Why it mattersRhat shows whether the simulation has settled on a stable answer; values well above 1 signal that it has not converged.
CheckWhether the paper reports a convergence/mixing diagnostic (Rhat / R-hat / potential scale reduction) and the reported values relative to 1.1.
PassThe paper reports Rhat (or an equivalent named mixing diagnostic) and states or shows it is at or below the cutoff for all parameters.
FlagThe paper reports Rhat above the cutoff, reports it for only a subset of parameters, or fits by MCMC without reporting any convergence diagnostic.
Thresholdcutoff 1.1; provenance: rule of thumb; borderline band: [1.05, 1.1]
Inside the bandAll reported Rhat at or below 1.05: PASS. Any parameter's Rhat in (1.05, 1.10]: PASS only if Rhat is reported for all parameters and the paper states convergence was reached; borderline flag mandatory. Any Rhat above 1.10, or Rhat given for only some parameters with the rest unstated: FLAG.
Further reading
  • Paul‐Christian Bürkner. (2017). <b>brms</b> : An <i>R</i> Package for Bayesian Multilevel Models Using <i>Stan</i>. Journal of Statistical Software, 80(1). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v080.i01, p. 11 (PDF pagination)
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, p. 352
MULTILEVEL-INFER-02severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: bayesian_mcmcsilent: flag

A Bayesian MCMC multilevel fit must run at least two independent chains.

In plain wordsA Bayesian model fit by simulation (MCMC) should run at least two independent simulation runs, called chains, and state how many it ran.
Why it mattersChecking convergence means seeing whether separate chains agree, and a single chain cannot reveal that it has missed part of the range of plausible parameter values.
CheckThe number of independent MCMC chains the paper states it ran.
PassThe paper states it ran at least two independent chains.
FlagThe paper states it ran a single chain, or fits by MCMC without stating the number of chains.
Thresholdcutoff 2; provenance: rule of thumb; borderline band: [2, 2]
Inside the band1 chain (or number unstated): FLAG. Exactly 2 chains: PASS (the minimum needed to assess mixing). 3 or more chains: PASS.
Further reading
  • Paul‐Christian Bürkner. (2017). <b>brms</b> : An <i>R</i> Package for Bayesian Multilevel Models Using <i>Stan</i>. Journal of Statistical Software, 80(1). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v080.i01, p. 19 (PDF pagination)
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, p. 356
MULTILEVEL-INFER-03severity if flagged: highlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: tscs_panelsilent: flag

A TSCS/panel analysis must apply and report at least one explicit correction for the nonspherical error structure of its data, since a multilevel specification does not supply that correction on its own. A single enumerated correction present anywhere is sufficient to PASS; total silence FLAGs.

In plain wordsA study of units observed repeatedly over time should report at least one correction for errors that are correlated over time or across units or that vary in size, such as a lagged outcome, a serial-correlation model, panel-corrected standard errors, or period indicators.
Why it mattersSuch data usually have these error patterns, and leaving them unaddressed gives incorrect standard errors; fitting a multilevel model does not fix this on its own.
CheckWhether the TSCS/panel paper reports an explicit correction for its error structure, from any of: a lagged dependent variable, an AR/Prais-Winsten/Cochrane-Orcutt transformation or explicit serial-correlation model, panel-corrected standard errors, time/period indicators for common shocks, or a stated heteroscedasticity correction.
PassThe paper reports at least one enumerated correction for its error structure (lagged dependent variable; AR/Prais-Winsten/Cochrane-Orcutt or explicit serial-correlation model; panel-corrected standard errors; time/period indicators for common shocks; or a stated heteroscedasticity correction) anywhere in the paper or its appendices.
FlagThe paper fits a TSCS/panel model (including a multilevel one) and reports no correction for any of serial correlation, contemporaneous correlation, or panel heteroscedasticity.
Further reading
  • Boris Shor, Joseph Bafumi, Luke Keele, & David S. Park. (2007). A Bayesian Multilevel Modeling Approach to Time-Series Cross-Sectional Data. Political Analysis, 15(2), 165-181. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpm006, pp. 171, 173

Robustness2

Sensitivity, diagnostics, and alternative specifications.

MULTILEVEL-ROBUST-01severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: multilevel_modelsilent: flag

A fitted multilevel model must be accompanied by at least one reported check of its fit to the data.

In plain wordsA paper that fits a multilevel model should report at least one check of how well the model fits the data, such as comparing the observed data with data simulated from the model, or plotting residuals.
Why it mattersA fitted model should not be accepted unexamined; comparing the real data with data the model generates shows whether the model describes the data well.
CheckWhether the paper reports a model-fit or adequacy check for the fitted multilevel model.
PassThe paper reports at least one fit/adequacy check from the corpus set, a posterior predictive check comparing observed to replicated data, residual or binned-residual plots, a comparison of model-implied to observed summaries, or a fake-data parameter-recovery check.
FlagThe paper reports a fitted multilevel model with no fit or adequacy check of any kind.
Further reading
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, pp. 513, 519
MULTILEVEL-ROBUST-02severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: count_binomial_multilevelsilent: flag

A Poisson/binomial grouped-count multilevel model must report an overdispersion check or an overdispersion-accommodating specification.

In plain wordsA multilevel model for counts or proportions (Poisson or binomial) should check for overdispersion, meaning more spread in the data than the model allows, or use a model that allows for it, such as a negative-binomial model.
Why it mattersThese models have no separate parameter for spread, so uncorrected overdispersion makes confidence intervals too narrow. Outcomes coded only 0 or 1 are exempt.
CheckWhether a grouped-count or binomial-proportion multilevel model reports an overdispersion check or an overdispersion-accommodating specification.
PassThe paper reports an overdispersion diagnostic (e.g. a dispersion statistic or test) or fits an overdispersion-accommodating specification from the corpus set, a negative-binomial or beta-binomial family, a quasi-likelihood dispersion adjustment, or an observation-level random effect.
FlagA Poisson/binomial grouped-count multilevel model is fit with neither an overdispersion check nor an overdispersion-accommodating specification.
Further reading
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, pp. 117, 325

Reporting3

What the paper must state for a reader to evaluate it.

MULTILEVEL-REPORT-01severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: mixed_model_predictionsilent: flag

Predictions from a mixed model must be labeled marginal or conditional on the random effects.

In plain wordsA paper should state whether each prediction from its mixed model is marginal (for the population as a whole) or conditional (specific to particular groups, using their estimated group-level deviations).
Why it mattersBecause the model lets groups differ, a prediction can describe the population average or a specific group, and readers need to know which one they are seeing.
CheckWhether reported predictions/fitted values are labeled as marginal/population-level or as conditional on particular groups' random effects.
PassThe paper states, for its reported predictions, whether they are marginal (population-level) or conditional on the estimated group random effects.
FlagThe paper presents predictions from the mixed model without stating whether they are marginal or group-conditional.
If flagged, the author can fix it byState for every predicted or fitted value whether it is a marginal (population-level, random effects at zero) prediction or a prediction conditional on a specific group's estimated random effects.
Further reading
  • Douglas M. Bates, Martin Mächler, Benjamin M. Bolker, & Steve Walker. (2015). Fitting Linear Mixed-Effects Models Using <b>lme4</b>. Journal of Statistical Software, 67(1). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v067.i01, p. 36 (PDF pagination)
MULTILEVEL-REPORT-02severity if flagged: lowhigh consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: multilevel_modelsilent: flag

A multilevel model must state the number of groups for each grouping factor.

In plain wordsA multilevel model should state how many groups there are for each grouping factor, for example how many countries when people are grouped by country.
Why it mattersHow reliable the estimates of variation between groups are depends on how many groups there are, though no fixed minimum number applies.
CheckWhether the paper states the number of groups (levels of each grouping factor) contributing to the model.
PassThe paper states the number of higher-level units for each grouping factor in the model (a count, or a table from which the count is directly readable).
FlagThe paper fits a multilevel model without stating how many groups each grouping factor has.
If flagged, the author can fix it byState the number of groups (levels) for each grouping factor in the model, so readers can judge how well the group-level variance and coefficients are identified.
Further reading
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, p. 275
  • Boris Shor, Joseph Bafumi, Luke Keele, & David S. Park. (2007). A Bayesian Multilevel Modeling Approach to Time-Series Cross-Sectional Data. Political Analysis, 15(2), 165-181. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpm006, p. 169
MULTILEVEL-REPORT-03severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: bayesian_mcmcsilent: flag

A Bayesian multilevel model must state the priors placed on its parameters.

In plain wordsA Bayesian multilevel model should state the priors, meaning the distributions assumed before seeing the data, placed on its parameters, including those for variation between groups.
Why it mattersEvery parameter in such a model has a prior, and software defaults are not guaranteed to be sensible, so silently relying on them is not enough.
CheckWhether the paper states the prior distributions placed on the model's parameters (including variance/hyperparameters), rather than leaving them unstated or as unnamed software defaults.
PassThe paper states the prior distributions used for its parameters (coefficients and variance/hyperparameters), whether chosen or explicitly adopted defaults it names.
FlagThe paper fits a Bayesian multilevel model without stating the priors used for its parameters.
Further reading
  • Paul‐Christian Bürkner. (2017). <b>brms</b> : An <i>R</i> Package for Bayesian Multilevel Models Using <i>Stan</i>. Journal of Statistical Software, 80(1). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v080.i01, p. 10 (PDF pagination)
  • Andrew Gelman & Jennifer Hill. (2006). Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models. Cambridge University Press eBooks. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511790942, p. 346
