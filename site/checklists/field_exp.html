The checklists

Field experiments

23 checks, each resting on claims from the methods literature. The 43 sources listed at the end are the ones cited as further reading. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

all
Every paper in the corpus.
bundled_treatment
Fires only if the paper describes its treatment arm as combining two or more distinct components. Near-miss: a single-component treatment with incidental delivery logistics does NOT fire it.
longitudinal_followup
Fires when the paper states outcomes were collected in a follow-up/endline wave after assignment, so enrolled units could fail to be re-measured. Does NOT fire when every assigned unit's outcome comes from a complete administrative record with no possible non-response.
imperfect_compliance
Fires when the paper states that some units assigned to treatment did not actually receive/take it (partial contact, uptake, or noncompliance). Does NOT fire when the paper states full compliance, and defaults OUT when the paper is silent about uptake.
matched_pair_audit
Fires when the paper states an in-person audit/tester design using paired human confederates who appear before the decision-maker. Does NOT fire for a correspondence study using fictitious written applications, where comparability of decision-maker-visible information is mechanical rather than achieved by matching people.
signal_manipulation
Fires only if the paper states its manipulation operates by sending a signal/cue/proxy for a trait (e.g. a name, price, or label standing in for a characteristic). Near-miss: directly assigning the substantive treatment good/service itself does NOT fire it.
iv_estimation
Fires when the paper states it uses an instrumental-variables second stage, including using random assignment as an instrument for treatment receipt or for a mediator/endogenous regressor. Does NOT fire when the paper reports only an intent-to-treat / reduced-form estimate with no instrumented quantity.
mediation_claim
Fires when the paper attributes its treatment effect to a specific named intermediate mechanism/mediator. Does NOT fire for a treatment-effect claim on a final outcome with no asserted mediator; a design that experimentally manipulates the mediator itself SATISFIES rather than triggers the concern.
heterogeneous_nonrandom_moderator
Fires when the paper reports treatment-effect heterogeneity, a subgroup contrast, or an interaction defined by a moderator variable it did NOT randomly assign. Does NOT fire for heterogeneity across a randomized factor (e.g. a randomized 2x2), nor for a single pooled average effect with no subgroup split.
voluntary_participation
Fires when the paper states subjects consented to or opted into enrollment (a recruited or volunteer sample). Does NOT fire when the intervention is applied to a pre-existing full population (e.g. every name on an administrative list) without an opt-in step.
has_control_group
Fires when the paper states a no-treatment or untreated comparison arm. Does NOT fire for a design with only active/alternative arms and no untreated control.
stratified_or_blocked
Fires when the paper states it stratified, blocked, matched, paired, or rerandomized on covariates prior to assignment. Does NOT fire for simple/complete randomization with no stated stratification, nor for covariate control introduced only at the analysis stage.
randomized_experiment
Fires when the paper states it assigned units to treatment/control by an explicit random procedure. Does NOT fire for an observational or 'as-if random' natural-experiment comparison the paper never randomized itself.
clustered_data
Fires when the paper states its randomization unit is a group/cluster, or that it has multiple correlated observations per unit (repeated measures). Does NOT fire merely because units sit on a geographic grid when the paper describes individual-level assignment and one observation per unit.
multiple_outcomes
Fires when the paper reports significance tests for two or more distinct outcome measures or subgroup contrasts. Does NOT fire for a single pre-declared primary outcome accompanied only by descriptive secondary statistics.
null_primary_result
Fires when the paper reports its primary treatment estimate as statistically non-significant and draws a substantive reading from that null. Does NOT fire when the primary effect is significant and only incidental secondary outcomes are null.
structural_model
Fires when the paper states it estimates a structural/parametric economic model (for counterfactual or policy simulation) beyond reduced-form effect estimation. Does NOT fire for a reduced-form regression that merely makes functional-form choices.
self_report_sensitive
Fires when the paper states its outcome is self-reported AND concerns sensitive/socially-undesirable behavior or attitudes, or that the treatment could change willingness to report. Does NOT fire for a self-reported but non-sensitive factual outcome, nor for an objective/administrative measure.
proxy_outcome
Fires only if the paper itself frames its outcome as a proxy, detected-trace, or discrepancy measure of the construct of interest (e.g. administrative traces of a hidden behavior, a coarse indicator standing in for the true outcome). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a direct measurement the paper treats as the construct itself, or the generic imperfection every measure has that the paper does not flag. Default OUT on silence.

Identification13

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must describe a genuine random assignment mechanism, its unit, and its probability scheme.

In plain wordsThe paper should explain how units were randomly assigned to treatment and control, what the unit of assignment was, and the chances of assignment to each group, unless equal chances are clear from the design.
Why it mattersA claim that an experiment shows cause and effect rests on a real random draw with known chances of assignment.
CheckThe methods section's description of how units were assigned to treatment and control, including the randomization unit and the assignment probabilities or scheme.
PassThe paper states that units were assigned to conditions by an explicit random procedure and gives the assignment unit; the probability scheme is stated or evident from the design (equal-probability assignment, a block/stratified draw, or explicitly stated probabilities).
FlagThe paper claims experimental identification but never describes a random assignment procedure, or leaves the assignment unit unstated. Leaving exact probabilities implicit where equal-probability assignment is evident is not by itself disqualifying.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-02severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: pass

The paper must not condition its estimate on any variable realized after treatment assignment.

In plain wordsThe paper should estimate the effect on everyone who was randomized, and should not pick its sample or controls using anything measured after assignment, such as who responded, who complied, or an intermediate outcome.
Why it mattersFiltering or adjusting on something the treatment could have changed undoes the randomization and lets other differences between groups creep back in.
CheckWhether the estimating sample or covariate set is defined by any quantity measured after assignment rather than fixed pre-assignment.
PassThe estimate is computed on the full randomized sample using only pre-assignment covariates; no analysis variable used to select the sample or adjust the estimate is realized after treatment.
FlagThe paper conditions its estimate on a post-treatment quantity (analyzing only responders/donors/survivors, splitting on a treatment-affected subgroup, or controlling for an intermediate outcome).
PrevalenceCoppock (2018) reports inappropriate conditioning on response appears in a sizable share of surveyed audit studies.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-03severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 12 sourcesapplies to: bundled_treatmentsilent: flag

When a single treatment arm combines multiple distinct components, the paper must not attribute the effect to one component unless a separate arm isolates it, and otherwise must state the attribution is indeterminate.

In plain wordsWhen one treatment bundles several distinct parts, the paper should credit the effect to a single part only if a separate arm isolates that part, and otherwise should say the responsible part cannot be identified.
Why it mattersWithout an arm that varies one part alone, the data cannot show which part produced the effect.
CheckWhether a treatment the paper describes as multi-component is either decomposed by a dedicated arm or accompanied by an explicit statement that the responsible component cannot be identified.
PassA dedicated arm varies the component of interest against an otherwise-identical alternative, or the paper explicitly states the effect cannot be attributed to any single bundled component.
FlagThe paper attributes the effect to one component of an admittedly bundled treatment without an isolating arm and without acknowledging the indeterminacy.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-04severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: longitudinal_followupsilent: flag

A paper with follow-up outcomes must assess whether attrition differs across arms.

In plain wordsWhen outcomes are measured in a later follow-up, the paper should check whether dropout differs between treatment and control, for example by comparing dropout rates or who dropped out, or by bounding the estimate.
Why it mattersIf treatment changes who stays in the study, the remaining groups are no longer comparable and the estimate is biased.
CheckWhether the paper reports a comparison of attrition/non-response rates (and ideally baseline composition of attriters) across treatment and control.
PassThe paper reports that attrition/non-response was compared across arms (rates and/or attriter characteristics) or bounds the estimate against differential attrition.
FlagThe paper collects follow-up outcomes but never checks whether attrition differs across arms.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-05severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: imperfect_compliancesilent: pass

Under noncompliance the paper must not estimate the effect from a naive as-treated comparison.

In plain wordsWhen some units did not receive the treatment they were assigned, the paper should base its main estimate on the randomized assignment, either comparing assigned groups (intent-to-treat) or using assignment as an instrument for receipt.
Why it mattersComparing those who actually took the treatment with those who did not mixes in the reasons people took it, bringing back selection bias.
CheckWhether the primary contrast is between randomized assignment arms (ITT, or assignment-instrumented compliance effect) rather than between units grouped by whether they actually received treatment.
PassThe primary effect is estimated from randomized assignment (ITT, or a compliance effect that instruments receipt with assignment).
FlagThe paper estimates the treatment effect by comparing units who actually received treatment against those who did not, without instrumenting receipt with assignment.

A matched-pair audit must state that testers were matched on all outcome-relevant traits except the manipulated one.

In plain wordsIn an in-person audit that sends pairs of testers, the paper should state that each pair was matched on all traits that could affect the outcome except the one being tested.
Why it mattersA difference in how testers were treated can be traced to the tested trait only if the testers were otherwise alike.
CheckWhether the paper describes matching or standardizing the paired testers on outcome-relevant characteristics apart from the manipulated trait.
PassThe paper states that paired testers were matched/standardized on the characteristics that could affect the outcome, leaving only the manipulated trait to differ.
FlagThe paper uses paired in-person confederates but does not describe matching them on outcome-relevant characteristics.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-07severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: signal_manipulationsilent: flag

When the manipulation operates through a signal or cue that could carry meaning beyond the intended trait, the paper must identify and empirically address the possibility that the signal conveys unintended, outcome-relevant attributes.

In plain wordsWhen the treatment works through a signal or cue, the paper should name other outcome-relevant traits the signal could convey and test or argue that they do not drive the result.
Why it mattersA cue meant to signal one trait may also signal others, and those could explain the measured difference.
CheckWhether the paper names the alternative attributes its manipulated signal could convey and tests or argues against them.
PassThe paper identifies the plausible unintended attributes the signal could convey and reports a test or explicit argument that they do not drive the measured difference.
FlagThe paper's manipulation relies on a signal/proxy and it neither names nor addresses the unintended attributes that signal could carry.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-08severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: iv_estimationsilent: flag

An IV estimate must state and defend the exclusion restriction for its instrument.

In plain wordsWhen the paper uses an instrumental variable (a factor used to shift the treatment, such as random assignment), it should state that the instrument affects the outcome only through the treatment and defend that claim.
Why it mattersIf the instrument reaches the outcome through another path, the estimate is invalid.
CheckWhether the paper states the exclusion restriction for its instrument and offers a defense of its plausibility.
PassThe paper states that the instrument influences the outcome only through the instrumented variable and gives a reasoned or empirical defense of that restriction.
FlagThe paper reports an IV estimate without stating the exclusion restriction, or states it without any defense of its plausibility.

A mechanism claim from a non-manipulated mediator must be presented as suggestive, not causal.

In plain wordsWhen the paper explains an effect through a mediator (an intervening factor) that it measured but did not experimentally manipulate, it should present that mechanism as suggestive rather than proven.

Heterogeneity across a non-randomized moderator must not be read as that moderator's isolated causal effect.

In plain wordsWhen the paper reports how effects differ across groups defined by a trait that was not randomly assigned, it should treat that difference as descriptive and not as the trait's own causal effect.
Why it mattersA trait that was not assigned at random travels with other traits, so the difference may reflect any of them.
CheckWhether a reported heterogeneity/interaction is defined by a non-randomized moderator, and whether the paper flags that contrast as non-causal/confounded.
PassThe paper presents heterogeneity across a non-randomized moderator as descriptive/confounded (a bundle of correlated traits), not as the moderator's isolated causal effect.
FlagThe paper interprets an interaction with a non-randomized moderator as that moderator's causal effect.

In plain wordsWhen participants chose or consented to join, the paper should address how they may differ from the population the claim covers, for example by comparing participants with non-participants or reasoning about how opt-in shapes the sample.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-12severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: has_control_groupsilent: flag

A paper with a control arm must address control access to substitutes and spillover onto controls.

In plain wordsThe paper should address whether control units could have obtained close substitutes for the treatment or been affected by treated units, by arguing or showing they were not, or by measuring that contamination.
Why it mattersIf controls received something like the treatment, they no longer show what happens without it, and the estimate is biased.
CheckWhether the paper addresses that its control units could have accessed close substitutes for the treatment or received spillover from treated units.
PassThe paper addresses control-group integrity (argues or shows controls lacked access to substitutes and were not exposed to treatment spillover, or measures/bounds that contamination).
FlagThe paper relies on a control arm without addressing whether controls could have obtained substitutes or received spillover.

FIELD_EXP-IDENT-13severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

Where subjects could have been aware of the study or their assignment, the paper must address whether that awareness could have altered the outcome; a manifestly covert/unobtrusive design satisfies this.

In plain wordsWhen subjects could have known they were studied or which group they were in, the paper should address whether that awareness, rather than the treatment, could explain the result; a clearly covert design meets this.
Why it mattersKnowing one is being studied can change behavior and distort the estimated effect.
CheckWhether the paper states subjects' awareness of the study/assignment and addresses whether that awareness (rather than the treatment) could have produced the measured response.
PassThe paper addresses observation/demand effects (delivered treatment covertly, argues awareness was equal across arms, or otherwise rules the artifact out), OR the design is manifestly unobtrusive/covert so that subjects were unaware they were being studied.
FlagSubjects were plausibly aware of the study or their assignment, or awareness could differ across arms or be induced by the treatment or its delivery, and the paper is silent on whether that awareness (rather than the treatment) could have produced the measured response.
If flagged, the author can fix it byState whether subjects were aware they were being studied or knew their assignment status, and if so, address how you rule out that awareness (Hawthorne/demand) rather than the treatment drove the measured response.

Estimation2

Whether the estimator recovers the target parameter.

FIELD_EXP-EST-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: stratified_or_blockedsilent: flag

A stratified/blocked/paired design should include its design variables in the analysis; omitting them yields wrong-size (overly conservative) inference.

In plain wordsWhen assignment was done within strata, blocks, or matched pairs (groups formed before randomizing), the paper should include those groupings in its analysis, such as a dummy for each pair or block.
Why it mattersLeaving them out keeps the estimate on target but makes standard errors the wrong size, too large.
CheckWhether the estimating specification includes the stratification/block/pair indicators used in assignment.
PassThe analysis includes the stratification variables as covariates (or a dummy for each matched pair / block).
FlagThe design stratified/blocked/paired on covariates but the analysis omits those design variables.

FIELD_EXP-EST-02severity if flagged: lowlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: randomized_experimentsilent: pass

Controls must not be selected on the basis of statistically significant baseline differences.

In plain wordsThe paper should choose control variables for their expected link to the outcome, and should not add or drop them because a baseline difference between groups was statistically significant.
Why it mattersWith valid randomization, baseline significance tests are a poor guide to controls; what matters is how well a covariate predicts the outcome.
CheckWhether the paper selects its analysis controls on the basis of statistically significant baseline imbalances.
PassThe paper does not choose controls from baseline significance tests (it adjusts for pre-specified outcome-predictive covariates regardless of imbalance significance, or its control choice is not driven by balance p-values).
FlagThe paper adds or drops controls specifically because a baseline covariate showed a statistically significant difference across arms.

Inference3

Standard errors, tests, and their validity.

FIELD_EXP-INFER-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: clustered_datasilent: flag

Clustered or repeated-observation designs must report correlation-adjusted standard errors.

In plain wordsWhen treatment is assigned to groups or units are observed more than once, the paper should report standard errors that allow for correlation within groups or units, for example by clustering at the assignment level.
Why it mattersTreating correlated observations as independent makes results look more precise than they are.
CheckThe stated inference method for the reported standard errors relative to the level of clustering / repeated observation.
PassThe paper reports inference that accounts for the correlation (standard errors clustered at the assignment level, a random-effects/GEE model, or aggregation to the independent unit).
FlagThe paper has clustered assignment or repeated observations per unit but reports standard errors that treat the correlated observations as independent.

In plain wordsWhen the main result is not statistically significant, the paper should interpret it in light of the study's power (its ability to detect an effect of a given size) and should not present it as proof of no effect or of equal outcomes across conditions.

Robustness1

Sensitivity, diagnostics, and alternative specifications.

FIELD_EXP-ROBUST-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: structural_modelsilent: flag

A structural model used for counterfactual claims must report sensitivity to its assumptions.

In plain wordsWhen the paper uses a structural model (a model built on explicit mathematical assumptions) to make counterfactual or policy claims, it should show how its estimates change under different assumptions.
Why it mattersThe model's estimates depend on those assumptions, so its conclusions are only as solid as their robustness to them.
CheckWhether the paper reports how its structural estimates respond to varying the key parametric/distributional/functional-form assumptions.
PassThe paper reports sensitivity analysis showing how the structural estimates move under alternative parametric or distributional assumptions.
FlagThe paper draws counterfactual/policy conclusions from a structural model without examining sensitivity to its assumptions.

FIELD_EXP-REPORT-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must state the scope conditions bounding generalization of its estimate.

In plain wordsThe paper should state the population, setting, and range of treatment over which its estimate holds, and name what limits extending it further.
Why it mattersAn experimental estimate reflects its own sample, setting, and range of treatment, so it may not carry over elsewhere.
CheckWhether the paper states the limits on generalizing its estimate (sample/setting selectivity, the induced range of treatment, single-channel or partial-equilibrium scope).
PassThe paper explicitly states the population/setting/range over which its estimate is claimed to hold and names what bounds extrapolation beyond it.
FlagThe paper generalizes its finding without stating the scope conditions that limit that generalization.

In plain wordsWhen some assigned units did not receive treatment, the paper should report, for each treatment arm, the share of assigned units that were actually treated or contacted.
