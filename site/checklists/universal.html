The checklists

Universal

28 checks that apply to every paper, whatever its research design. The 87 chapters and articles listed at the end are cited as further reading for them; they come from works on at least two graduate syllabi that state requirements for any research design. The checks were built from them by the same procedure as the method checklists. Each is supported by at least three works, and the list carries no consensus grade. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

covariate_adjusted
Does the paper state that it controls for, adjusts for, conditions on, or holds constant one or more variables when it produces an estimate or comparison that it describes in causal terms? Phrases such as 'controlling for', 'adjusting for', or 'we include X as a control' fire it. Near-misses that do NOT fire: covariates shown only in a descriptive or balance table; adjusted associations that the paper explicitly labels non-causal; random intercepts or grouping terms that the paper does not describe as controls. Decided from the paper's words; ambiguous is NA. A fired gate on a silent paper takes verdict_when_silent; unfired or ambiguous is NA.
quant_causal_estimate
Fires only if the paper reports a numerical estimate that it explicitly calls an effect, impact, or causal effect of a named cause on an outcome. Does NOT fire on regression coefficients the paper describes only as associations or correlations. Does NOT fire on descriptive statistics. Unfired or ambiguous means NA.
causal_claim
Does the paper explicitly state, as its own finding or estimate, that one factor causes, affects, has an effect or impact on, produces, or leads to an outcome? Fires only on such wording applied to the paper's own result. Near-misses that do NOT fire: 'associated with', 'correlated with', 'predicts', or 'linked to' wording; causal claims attributed only to prior literature; hypotheses in a theory section that the paper does not claim to have shown. Decide from the paper's words alone. If unclear, the trigger has not fired and gated checks are NA. A fired trigger on a paper silent about a check's requirement takes that check's verdict_when_silent.
before_after_comparison
Does the paper state that its causal evidence is a comparison of the same units' outcomes before versus after the treatment or event? Near-misses that do NOT fire: data observed over time where the paper's stated comparison is between treated and untreated units at the same time; a pre-period value used only as a control variable. Decide from the paper's words alone. Unclear means not fired (NA).
observational_causal
Fires when causal_claim fires AND the paper does not state that the values of its key explanatory variable were set by a random mechanism (researcher randomization, lottery, random draw). Descriptions such as 'as-if random', 'natural experiment', 'exogenous variation' or 'quasi-experimental' are NOT statements of random assignment, so such papers fire. Near miss that does NOT fire: a study that explicitly describes its key explanatory variable as randomly assigned. If it is unclear whether a stated randomization applies to the key explanatory variable, NA.
own_causal_effect
Does the paper explicitly call one of its own estimates or findings a causal effect? It fires when the paper uses words such as 'effect of', 'impact of', 'causes', 'leads to', or 'causal' for its own result, or says it estimates an effect of a treatment or exposure. It does NOT fire when the paper uses only 'associated with', 'correlated with', or 'predicts'. It does NOT fire when the paper only discusses causal claims made by other studies, or when it states that its estimates should not be read causally. If it is unclear whether the paper's own result is presented as causal, the trigger is not fired (NA).
nonexperimental_causal
causal_claim fires AND the paper states that the treatment or exposure was not assigned by the researchers AND the paper does not state that it was set by a random draw (a randomized trial or lottery, run by anyone). Qualifying statements describe the data as observational, administrative, survey-based, or naturally occurring, or say treatment was chosen by units, officials, or circumstances. Near-miss that does NOT fire: the paper is silent on who set treatment. If the source of treatment is ambiguous, NA.
reported_imbalance
Fires when the paper itself states that a named pre-treatment covariate differs between its treatment groups, calling it imbalanced, significantly different, or a notable difference. Does NOT fire when a coder computes a difference from a table the paper does not comment on. Ambiguity resolves to NA.
numerical_estimate
Does the paper report at least one numerical estimate of an effect or association? Near-miss that does NOT fire: qualitative case narratives; counts or frequencies reported only to describe the sample. Ambiguous resolves to not fired (NA).
estimate_as_finding
Does the paper report at least one numerical estimate that it presents as a finding, such as a coefficient, difference, proportion, rate, or correlation? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a table of sample counts or case listings used only to describe the data.
attenuation_argument
Does the paper explicitly state that measurement error biases one of its estimates toward zero, makes it conservative, or makes it a lower bound? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a generic remark that the data contain measurement error with no claim about the direction of bias. Ambiguous -> NA.
quantitative_estimates
Fires if the paper reports numeric estimates of a relationship between variables from a stated statistical model, such as a coefficient, a difference in means with uncertainty, or an odds ratio. Does NOT fire for purely descriptive counts, percentages, or frequency tables with no estimated relationship. Decided from the paper's words alone. Ambiguous cases are NA. A fired trigger on a silent paper takes verdict_when_silent.
reports_inferential_statistics
Does the paper report standard errors, confidence or credible intervals, p-values, significance markers, or test statistics for its own estimates? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a sentence saying a prior study's effect was significant. Ambiguity resolves to NA.
all
Every paper, whatever its design. No trigger to decide.
grouped_data
Fires if either holds. (1) The paper's own description of its key explanatory variable or treatment identifies it as defined, assigned, or measured at a group level (e.g., 'state policy', 'school-level treatment', 'village assignment'), while outcomes are measured on units within those groups. (2) The paper states that it sampled whole groups (schools, villages, firms, households) rather than individuals. Does NOT fire on: group fixed effects or dummies alone; cluster-robust standard errors appearing in a table note without such a description; repeated observations of the same unit (see repeated_units); spatial grid cells. Decided from the paper's own description. If unclear, the check is NA.
repeated_units
Does the paper state that it observes the same units (people, firms, states, countries, groups) in two or more time periods and pools those periods in one analysis? Does NOT fire on: repeated cross-sections of different respondents; a single post-treatment measurement; one unit followed over time (see linked_observations). Decided from the paper's own words. If unclear, the check is NA.
linked_observations
Does the paper itself describe its observations as linked by interaction, diffusion, contagion, competition, spatial adjacency, or network ties, or describe its data as a time series of a single unit? Does NOT fire on: a coder's suspicion that units are spatially or socially correlated when the paper does not say so; group or panel structure, which is covered by grouped_data and repeated_units. If unclear, the check is NA.
reports_uncertainty
Does the paper report standard errors, confidence or credible intervals, or p-values or significance markers for estimates it computed itself? Does NOT fire: uncertainty figures quoted from other studies; tables of point estimates with no uncertainty. The coder decides from the paper's words alone. If unclear, the check is NA. If fired and the paper is silent, verdict_when_silent applies.
quant_estimates
Does the paper report at least one numeric estimate of a relationship between variables (a coefficient, a difference in means or proportions, an odds ratio, an effect size)? Near miss that does NOT fire: only univariate descriptive statistics or counts of cases.
empirical_paper
Every empirical paper. No trigger to decide; the check always applies.
generalization_claim
Fires when the paper explicitly states that its findings apply to units, a population, or settings beyond the units it actually analyzed. Does NOT fire for: a description of the sample's characteristics; a limitations sentence saying results may not generalize; or policy implications offered only for the units studied. Ambiguous means NA.
proxy_measure
Does the paper explicitly describe one of its variables or codings as an indicator, proxy, index, measure, or operationalization of a named concept different from the recorded quantity itself? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a variable reported as exactly what it records (vote share analyzed as vote share, population as population) with no claim that it stands for a broader concept. Ambiguous -> NA.
typology
Does the paper explicitly present a typology, a classification of cases into named types or categories, or a periodization? Does NOT fire: a list of examples, factors, or mechanisms that is not presented as categories of cases.
convergent_validation
Does the paper explicitly present agreement or correlation between its measure and another measure of the same concept as evidence that its measure is valid? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a correlation matrix of variables reported without the paper calling it evidence of validity. Ambiguous -> NA.
nomological_validation
Does the paper explicitly present the association between its measure and a variable the concept is expected to cause or be caused by as evidence that its measure is valid? Near-miss that does NOT fire: the paper's main causal analysis, when the paper does not present it as validating the measure. Ambiguous -> NA.
cross_context_comparison
Does the paper explicitly compare levels or effects of the same measure across two or more named countries, cultures, language groups, historical periods, or demographic groups? Near-miss that does NOT fire: a pooled sample that spans countries or groups where the paper makes no comparison claim across them. Ambiguous -> NA.
borrowed_validation
Does the paper explicitly support its measure by citing validation, reliability, or equivalence evidence obtained on other cases, samples, countries, or periods? Near-miss that does NOT fire: citing the original source of a dataset without invoking any validation evidence from it. Ambiguous -> NA.
context_adjusted_measure
Does the paper explicitly state that it uses different indicators, different domains of observation, or adjusted versions of a common indicator (per-capita, context-normed) for different units or contexts? Near-miss that does NOT fire: routine unit conversion or inflation adjustment the paper does not present as adapting the measure to context. Ambiguous -> NA.
new_or_redefined_concept
Fires when the paper explicitly states that it introduces a new term or concept, or proposes its own definition that departs from or refines an existing one. Does NOT fire for adopting a cited definition unchanged. Ambiguous wording means NA.
interested_source
Does the paper explicitly state that its evidence comes from accounts by the actors whose conduct or performance is under study (self-reports about own behavior, officials' or agencies' figures on their own performance, participants' memoirs or accounts)? Near-miss that does NOT fire: survey questions about attitudes or demographics that the paper does not describe as reports on the respondents' own conduct. Ambiguous -> NA.
empirical_claim
Does the paper report empirical findings of its own, either quantitative estimates or qualitative evidence about cases? Does NOT fire: purely formal, theoretical, normative, or review papers that present no evidence of their own.

Identification5

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

UNIV-IDENT-04severity if flagged: highapplies to: covariate_adjustedsilent: pass

A paper must not read a total effect from a single-step adjustment that includes variables it identifies as downstream of the treatment.

In plain wordsThe paper should not call its estimate the total effect if it adjusts in one step for a variable it describes as caused by the treatment, as a pathway to the outcome, or as measured after treatment began; it should call it a direct effect or use a multi-stage method.
Why it mattersHolding fixed something between cause and outcome removes part of the effect, leaving only a direct effect.
CheckThe list of adjustment variables, the paper's own description of their timing or causal role, and how it labels the resulting estimate.
PassThe paper describes none of its adjustment variables as caused by the treatment, as a mechanism or mediator, or as measured after treatment began. Alternatively, where such a variable is included, the paper labels the estimate a direct or controlled effect rather than the total effect, or uses an explicitly multi-stage procedure for it.
FlagThe paper includes in a single-step adjustment a variable it itself describes as a consequence of the treatment, a mediator, or measured after treatment onset, and interprets the treatment estimate as the total effect.

A paper reporting an effect estimate must state and support its position on spillovers between units.

In plain wordsThe paper should say whether one unit's outcome could be affected by other units' exposure to the cause (spillovers), and give an argument or analysis for that position.
Why it mattersEffect estimates assume units do not affect each other; spillovers break the comparison between groups and can bias the result, not just make it less precise.
CheckA statement about whether a unit's outcome could be affected by other units' exposure.
PassPASS if both hold: (a) the paper explicitly states whether units' outcomes could be affected by other units' exposure to the cause; and (b) it gives an argument or analysis supporting that position.
FlagFLAG if either holds: (a) the paper makes no such statement; or (b) spillovers are acknowledged without argument or analysis.

UNIV-IDENT-17severity if flagged: highapplies to: before_after_comparison, causal_claimsilent: flag

Before-versus-after causal evidence must either show the outcome's pre-treatment trajectory or explicitly state and support the assumption that the outcome would not have changed without treatment.

In plain wordsA before-versus-after comparison should show the outcome at two or more points before treatment, or state, with a reason, that without treatment the outcome would have stayed at its earlier level with no carryover from earlier periods.
Why it mattersThe earlier value stands in for the outcome without treatment, which fails if the outcome was already moving, units change on their own, or past exposure carries over.
CheckWhether the paper reports the outcome's trajectory before treatment, or explicitly states and supports the assumption that outcomes would have stayed at their pre-treatment level absent treatment.
PassEither (a) the paper reports outcome values, or an estimated trend, at two or more distinct time points before treatment; or (b) the paper explicitly states the assumption that, without treatment, the units' outcomes would have remained at their pre-treatment level with no carryover from earlier periods, and gives at least one reason for that assumption.
FlagThe paper reports the outcome at one or zero pre-treatment time points and does not state, with a reason, the assumption that outcomes would have remained at their pre-treatment level without treatment.

UNIV-IDENT-28severity if flagged: mediumapplies to: own_causal_effectsilent: flag

Define the causal contrast: the applicable action, the alternative it is compared with, and when the outcome is measured after treatment begins.

In plain wordsThe paper should define its causal comparison: an action that could be applied, the alternative it is compared with, and when the outcome is measured after treatment starts. A fixed trait such as race is not itself an action, though a signal or policy tied to it can be.
Why it mattersA causal effect is defined by contrasting an applicable action with a named alternative at a stated time.
CheckStatements of (1) the real or hypothetical action that puts a unit in the treatment condition, (2) the comparison condition, and (3) when the outcome is measured relative to the start of treatment.
PassAll three elements appear somewhere in the paper. (1) The treatment is described as an action, policy, program, dose, or hypothetical manipulation that could be applied to the units. (2) The alternative condition is named, for example no program, usual practice, a placebo, or a lower dose. (3) The outcome's measurement time or follow-up period relative to treatment start is stated. If the paper claims a causal effect of a characteristic it names as the unit's own fixed attribute (race, sex, ethnicity, birth cohort), element (1) is met only if the described action works on something manipulable connected to the attribute, such as a signal, a perception, or a policy, rather than on the attribute itself.
FlagAny of elements (1)-(3) is missing, or the only cause described is a fixed attribute of the units with no manipulable element identified.

UNIV-IDENT-30severity if flagged: highapplies to: nonexperimental_causalsilent: flag

Name the assumptions that make the non-experimental estimate causal, and give a reason for each.

In plain wordsThe paper should name the assumptions that let its non-experimental estimate be read as causal, such as parallel trends or no unmeasured confounding, and support each one with an argument, an institutional fact, outside evidence, or a diagnostic test.
Why it mattersWithout random assignment, a causal reading rests on assumptions the data cannot supply, so each needs support rather than being taken for granted.
Check(1) The identifying assumption or assumptions the paper presents as the basis for reading its main estimate causally. (2) For each one, any justification given with it.
PassBoth parts hold. (1) At least one identifying assumption is named as the basis for the causal reading. Examples: no unmeasured confounding given named covariates, parallel trends, an exclusion restriction, continuity at a cutoff, stability of the estimated relationship under intervention, or an explicit account of why treatment variation is as good as random. Calling a variable 'exogenous' counts only if the paper says what it is independent of or uncorrelated with (which unobservables, or which equation's error term). (2) Every assumption named under (1) carries at least one justification of these kinds: (a) a substantive or subject-matter argument; (b) an institutional or procedural fact about how treatment arose; (c) external data or a prior study; (d) a reported diagnostic. Where the named assumption is no unmeasured confounding given covariates, a stated reason for the covariate choice (the covariates affect the treatment decision or the outcome) counts as (a). An unadjusted treated-versus-untreated difference labelled causal is judged by the same two parts.
FlagAny one of the following produces FLAG. No identifying assumption is named. 'Exogenous' is used without saying with respect to what, and no other assumption is named. At least one named assumption has no justification of type (a)-(d); a paper that justifies one named assumption but not another is FLAG.

UNIV-EST-04severity if flagged: mediumapplies to: causal_claimsilent: pass

A paper must not convert an average effect into unit-level or distributional claims without a stated added assumption.

In plain wordsThe paper should not use an average effect to claim effects on every unit, on a specific case, or on the spread of individual effects unless it states an added assumption that supports the claim.
Why it mattersEffects on individual units cannot be computed, and an average effect does not show that effects are uniform or how they are spread.
CheckClaims the paper derives from an average or group-level effect.
PassEither (a) the paper's claims stay at the level of the average, or (b) every individual-level or distributional claim is accompanied by a stated additional assumption supporting it.
FlagOn the basis of an average or group-level effect, and without stating a supporting assumption, the paper does at least one of: (a) claims the treatment affected every unit; (b) claims it affected a specific named unit, or explains a specific case's outcome; (c) reports the median of unit-level effects, the share of units with a positive effect, or the variance of unit-level effects. A reported difference in outcome medians or quantiles does not count under (c) unless the paper describes it as a feature of the unit-level effects.
