The checklists

Measurement

62 checks, each resting on claims from the methods literature. The 16 sources listed at the end are the ones cited as further reading. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

all
Applies to every paper that reports a quantitative measure of anything. No trigger question; never NA.
external_comparison_validation
Does the paper state that it supports its measure by comparing it with something outside the measure itself, an official record, a criterion variable, an established alternative instrument, expert judgements, or a benchmark it names? Fires on that stated comparison-as-support. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting a correlation among its own items, or a descriptive correlation the paper does not offer as support for the measure. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
nomological_validation
Does the paper state that its measure is supported because it reproduces an expected, known, or theoretically predicted relationship with another variable? Fires on the paper offering that relationship as evidence for the measure. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting a substantive result that happens to match theory, without presenting it as validation of the measure. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
single_item_measure
Does the paper state that one question, one indicator, or one coded variable stands for a named concept in its analysis? Fires on the paper's own description of the operationalization (it names the concept and points to a single item). Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a battery whose items are also reported separately, or a single variable that is a direct physical count rather than a stand-in for an abstract concept. Ambiguous cases are NA.
automated_text_measure
Does the paper state that a variable was produced from text by a computer procedure, counting words, classifying documents, fitting a topic or scaling model, applying a language model? Fires on the paper's own description of the procedure. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: people reading and coding the text by hand, or text used only as illustration. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
supervised_text_classifier
Does the paper state that it hand-labelled a set of documents and used them to train a procedure that then labelled the rest? Fires on that stated training-set-plus-classifier design. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a topic model or other procedure the paper describes as needing no labels, or a dictionary/word-list count. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
unsupervised_text_categories
Does the paper state that categories, topics, or clusters were discovered by a model rather than fixed in advance? Fires on the paper naming such a method or describing the categories as estimated from the data. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a classifier trained on a predefined scheme, or an exploratory factor analysis of survey items (not text). Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
dictionary_measure
Does the paper state that it scored text by counting words from a list, lexicon, dictionary, or keyword set? Fires on that self-description, whether the list is borrowed or purpose-built. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a trained classifier whose features happen to be words, or a hand-coding scheme that mentions keywords as guidance. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
text_scaling
Does the paper state that it placed actors, documents, or units on a latent dimension estimated from text, an ideology score, a position, a one-dimensional scale from word data? Fires on that self-description. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: classifying documents into categories, or scaling roll-call votes or survey items rather than text. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
latent_variable_model
Does the paper state that it fitted a measurement model with unobserved variables, factor analysis, structural equation model, item-response model, latent class or latent trait model? Fires on the paper naming such a model. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a principal-components summary the paper does not describe as a latent-variable model, random intercepts used as a nuisance term, or a simple index. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
fitted_measure_downstream
Does the paper state that a variable entering a subsequent analysis is the output of a model it fitted, factor scores, latent estimates, classifier predictions, topic proportions, or another model-produced value? Does NOT fire: a directly recorded variable; a simple sum or average of items (that fires sum_or_average_score); a published index used as given without the paper stating it fitted the model. Unclear whether the value came from a fitted model: NA.
alpha_reported
Does the paper report a coefficient alpha / Cronbach's alpha figure for a set of items? Fires on the reported number or the paper's statement that alpha was computed. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting inter-coder agreement, omega, composite reliability, or a test-retest correlation. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
sum_or_average_score
Does the paper state that the combined variable is a sum, total, count, or unweighted mean of its items? Fires only where the paper names that arithmetic. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a factor score, an item-response score, a weighted index, or a composite whose rule the paper never states (the unstated case is REPORT-08, not this). Ambiguous cases are NA.
established_instrument
Does the paper say it used an instrument that already exists, naming it, citing its source publication, or calling it established, standard, validated, or previously used? Near-miss that does NOT fire it: writing items the paper describes as its own, or reusing a variable from a public dataset without naming an instrument. Ambiguous cases are out of scope (NA), never FLAG.
mcmc_estimated
Fires when the paper states that its estimates come from a Markov chain Monte Carlo, Gibbs, Hamiltonian, or other posterior sampler. Near-miss: naming a Bayesian prior in a model fitted by optimisation does not fire it. Unclear how the model was fitted: NA.
multi_item_scale
Does the paper state that two or more items, indicators, or component sub-measures are combined into one variable it then analyses? Fires on the paper's own description of building a scale, index, factor score, or composite. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting several related variables side by side without combining them, or entering several indicators as separate regressors. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
unsupervised_text
Does the paper state that it used a procedure that discovers categories or dimensions from its texts without labels fixed in advance, a topic model, a clustering, an unsupervised scaling? Does NOT fire: a paper stating its categories were fixed in advance and applied by coders or a trained classifier. Unclear: NA.
conjunctive_construct
Does the paper state that its concept requires all of its named parts to be present together, that the parts are necessary conditions, defining attributes, or that a case lacking one does not have the concept? Fires only on that explicit statement about the concept's structure (see REPORT-15, which asks any paper combining several components to make the statement). Near-miss that does NOT fire it: listing several dimensions of a concept without saying whether they can substitute for one another. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
standardized_before_aggregation
Does the paper state that component variables were standardized, z-scored, normalized, or rescaled to a common range before being combined? Fires on the paper's own description of the rescaling step. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting standardized coefficients in a regression table, or reporting items that happen to share a response scale. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
ordinal_as_interval
Does the paper state that a variable it treats numerically is made of ranked or ordered categories, a rank, a graded classification, an ordinal index, a Likert item entered as a number? Fires on the paper's own description of the variable's categories. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a continuous measurement (money, counts, time) or a variable whose category structure the paper never describes. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
estimated_measure_downstream
Does the paper state that a variable entering its substantive analysis is itself an estimate produced by a model, a classifier, a scaling procedure, or a set of factor scores? Fires on the paper's own statement that the variable was estimated or predicted (see REPORT-02, which asks every paper to disclose which of its analysed values a model produced). Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a directly recorded variable, or a simple sum of observed items. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
estimated_confounder
Fires when the paper states that a control variable in its analysis is such a model output. Near-miss: a recorded covariate, however imperfect, does not fire it. Unclear: NA.
declared_cutoff
Fires when the paper states that it applied a numeric cutoff to build its measure, a threshold for membership, for dichotomising, or for classifying. Near-miss: a conventional significance level, or a cutoff in a robustness appendix that does not produce the analysed measure, does not fire it. Unclear whether a cutoff produced the measure: NA.
reference_text_scaling
Does the paper state that the text scale's endpoints or reference points were set by designated anchor, training, or reference documents whose positions the analyst supplied? Fires on the paper naming those anchor documents. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a scaling model the paper describes as estimating positions without supplied anchors. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
cross_unit_latent_comparison
Does the paper state that it compares estimated latent scores across groups, periods, chambers, or item sets, for instance saying that units moved, that one group sits higher than another, or that scores from different periods are put on one scale? Fires on that stated comparison of estimated scores. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: reporting scores within a single group and period, or comparing directly observed variables. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
cross_context_measure
Does the paper state that one measurement instrument was applied across settings it describes as different, several countries, languages, historical periods, or distinct populations? Fires on the paper's own description of the spread of its cases. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a large sample drawn from one setting, or several settings measured with instruments the paper describes as separate and unconnected. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
administered_instrument
Does the paper state that it put questions, items, tasks, or stimuli to respondents or subjects and recorded their answers? Fires on the paper's own description of fielding an instrument (a survey battery, a questionnaire, an experimental task producing a recorded response). Near-miss that does NOT fire it: analysing an existing survey file, administrative records, or text produced for other purposes, without the paper saying it administered anything itself. If it is unclear whether the paper administered its own instrument, the trigger does not fire and every check gated on it is NA; a coder therefore never chooses between NA and FLAG for the same paper.
modified_instrument
Does the paper state that it changed an existing instrument, dropping, adding, or rewording items, altering response options, or rescoring it? Does NOT fire: administering a published instrument as originally specified; reporting a published instrument's own alternative short form as published. Unclear whether a change was made: NA.
new_instrument
Does the paper state that it created the measure itself, new items, a new coding scheme, a new index built for this study? Near-miss that does NOT fire it: adapting a named existing instrument (that is `established_instrument` plus REPORT-10), or assembling variables that already exist in a dataset. Ambiguity resolves out: NA, not FLAG.
human_coding
Does the paper state that people read, watched, or listened to material and assigned codes or categories to it for this study? Fires on the paper's own description of coders, coding, or content analysis. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: survey respondents answering about themselves, experts rating units on a scale (that is `expert_ratings`), or the paper using someone else's already-coded dataset without coding anything itself. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
supervised_classifier
Does the paper state that it trained a classifier or learner on cases it labelled by hand and applied it to unlabelled cases? Does NOT fire: a regression predicting a substantive outcome; applying a model built by someone else without stating that this paper trained one (that fires transported_instrument); a dictionary applied without training. Unclear: NA.
expert_or_coder_ratings
Fires when the paper states that the values it analyses were assigned by two or more human raters, coders, or experts judging units. Near-miss: a single author coding all cases alone, or survey respondents answering about themselves, does not fire it. Unclear how many people assigned the values: NA.
expert_ratings
Does the paper state that its values come from experts, judges, or raters scoring units on a response scale, either ratings it collected or a named expert-survey dataset? Fires on that self-description. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a single author coding cases, or coders applying a categorical codebook to documents (that is `human_coding`). Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
cross_validation_reported
Does the paper state that performance was estimated by cross-validation, repeated splitting, or a held-out fold procedure? Fires on that stated procedure. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: a single train/test split reported as such (that is covered by IDENT-10), or accuracy reported with no stated estimation procedure. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.
case_level_coding
Does the paper state that it assigned scores to a set of named cases (countries, parties, episodes, organizations) on the basis of case knowledge, historical or secondary sources? Fires on the paper's own description of case-by-case coding. Near-miss that does NOT fire it: coding a large sample of anonymous documents or respondents (that is `human_coding`), or importing a published country-year index without coding cases itself. Ambiguity resolves out to NA.

Identification16

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 7 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

A paper must support its main measured variable with more than one kind of evidence that it captures the concept it is named after.

In plain wordsThe paper should back its main measured variable with at least two different kinds of evidence that it captures the concept it is named after, such as comparison with an outside record or a test of a predicted relationship, reported or cited from specific studies.
Why it mattersEach kind of evidence covers only part of the case, and no design or sample size can fix a measure that misses its target.
CheckHow many distinct kinds of evidence the paper reports, or cites a named source as reporting, for the claim that its principal measured variable stands for the concept it is named after.
PassFor its main measured construct the paper reports at least two distinct kinds from this list, or cites specific studies reporting them: a comparison of the measure against an outside record or criterion variable; a stated check of the instrument's content against the paper's own definition; agreement with a different instrument for the same concept; a test of an association the theory predicts; a fitted measurement model for the item set; human checking of machine-assigned values; re-examination of individual cases' scores against knowledge of those cases. Evidence in an appendix, supplement, footnote, or replication archive counts exactly as main-text evidence does.
FlagThe paper names a construct and analyses values for it while reporting fewer than two of the enumerated kinds anywhere and citing no source that reports them. Reporting none of them, and resting the whole case on a single kind, both take FLAG; the reviewer records which of the two applies.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-02severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 5 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: pass

A paper must not offer reliability or agreement statistics as evidence that its measure captures the intended construct.

In plain wordsThe paper should not offer reliability or agreement figures, such as coder agreement or how well items hang together, as evidence that its measure captures the intended concept.
Why it mattersConsistency shows only that a procedure repeats itself; coders who share the same mistake can agree with each other and still be wrong together.
CheckWhether the paper's stated support for the claim that its measure captures the construct is a reliability, agreement, or internal-consistency figure.
PassNo reliability, inter-coder agreement, test-retest, or internal-consistency statistic is offered in the paper as support for the claim that the measure captures its construct. A paper that reports such statistics only as reliability evidence passes.
FlagThe paper's stated ground for the construct claim is such a statistic, for example that the measure is valid because coders agreed, because the items cohere, or because repeated administration gave the same answer.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-03severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: external_comparison_validationsilent: flag

A paper validating against an external standard must give evidence about that standard's own measurement quality.

In plain wordsWhen the paper checks its measure against an outside standard, it should say how well that standard is itself measured, for example its reliability, where it comes from and its known errors, or that it is an official record of the event itself.
Why it mattersThe comparison rests on the standard, so weakness in the standard can undo the case for the measure.
CheckWhether the paper says anything about how well the comparison variable it validates against is itself measured.
PassThe paper states something about the comparison standard's own measurement quality, a reliability figure for it, a statement of its provenance and error properties, or a cited source giving these. An explicit statement that the standard is an administrative record of the event itself also passes.
FlagThe paper names a criterion, record, or alternative instrument and reports the comparison without any statement about how well that comparison variable is measured.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-04severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: external_comparison_validationsilent: flag

A paper offering agreement with another instrument as validity evidence must state the conceptual grounds on which agreement was expected.

In plain wordsWhen the paper uses agreement with another measure as support, it should state the conceptual reason it expected the two to agree, or, if it claims to capture something others miss, what difference it expected and why.
Why it mattersWithout a stated expectation, any correlation could be read as good news, and close agreement counts against a measure said to capture what others miss.
CheckWhether the paper states a conceptual reason for expecting the agreement it reports with another instrument.
PassAlongside the reported agreement the paper states why, on conceptual grounds, the two instruments should line up, or, where it claims its measure catches something the others miss, states what divergence it expected and why. The statement may sit anywhere in the paper or its materials; the coder judges whether it is there, not when it was written.
FlagThe paper reports a correlation or agreement with another instrument as support for its measure with no stated conceptual expectation, so that any observed correspondence would have been read as favourable.
Further reading

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-05severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: nomological_validationsilent: pass

A paper must not report as a finding the same relationship it assumed in order to validate its measure.

In plain wordsThe paper should not present as a finding the same relationship it assumed in order to show its measure works, unless it says plainly that this relationship was assumed and is not offered as a finding.
Why it mattersA relationship taken as true to vouch for a measure cannot later be reported as something the measure discovered.
CheckWhether the relationship the paper used to vouch for its measure reappears among the paper's own reported findings.
PassThe relationship invoked to support the measure is not also presented as a result of the paper; or the paper states explicitly that this relationship was assumed for validation and therefore is not offered as a finding.
FlagThe same relationship is used both as the ground for accepting the measure and as a substantive finding, with no statement that the two roles conflict.
Further reading
  • Robert Adcock, David Collier (2001) Measurement Validity: A Shared Standard for Qualitative and Quantitative Research. American Political Science Review. https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055401003100, p. 543
MEASUREMENT-IDENT-06severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: single_item_measuresilent: flag

A paper measuring a concept with one item must report that item's error or defend the single-item choice.

In plain wordsWhen one question stands for a concept, the paper should report how much error that question carries, argue why one question is enough, or state the resulting limit on its conclusions.
Why it mattersOne question can stand for a concept only if the two move together almost exactly; for many-sided topics they do not, and about half of a single item's variation is error even in well-run surveys.
CheckWhether the paper says anything about the error carried by the single item it uses for a named concept.
PassThe paper reports an estimate of the item's reliability or error variance, or argues why one question suffices for this particular concept, or states the resulting limitation on the inferences it draws from that item.
FlagA single item carries a named concept through the analysis with no reliability estimate, no argument that one question suffices, and no stated limitation.

  • Stephen Ansolabehere, Jonathan Rodden, James M. Snyder (2008) The Strength of Issues: Using Multiple Measures to Gauge Preference Stability, Ideological Constraint, and Issue Voting. American Political Science Review. https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055408080210, pp. 220, 229

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-07severity if flagged: highmedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: automated_text_measuresilent: flag

A paper using an automated text measure must report a validation of that measure on its own corpus.

In plain wordsWhen the paper measures something from text automatically, it should check that measure on its own documents, for example by hand-checking a sample or comparing with independently coded values, and report what the check found.
Why it mattersHow a text method performs on one set of documents says nothing about the next, so its record in other studies does not show it works here.
CheckWhether the paper reports a validation of its text-derived measure carried out on the corpus it actually analyses.
PassThe paper reports a validation exercise performed on its own corpus, hand-checking a sample of the machine output, comparison with independently coded values for the same documents, or a stated comparison of the measure against known facts about those documents, and reports what that exercise found. Where the procedure is of a kind governed by IDENT-10, IDENT-11, IDENT-12, or IDENT-13, that more specific check carries the verdict and this one passes, so a single missing validation is recorded once rather than twice.
FlagA text-derived variable is analysed with no validation on the paper's own corpus and none of IDENT-10 to IDENT-13 applies to the procedure used; or the measure is supported only by citing the method's performance elsewhere.
Further reading
  • Justin Grimmer, Brandon Stewart (2013) Text as Data: The Promise and Pitfalls of Automatic Content Analysis Methods for Political Texts. Political Analysis. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mps028, pp. 268, 271

A paper measuring from text states the interpretive setting in which its texts were read.

In plain wordsWhen the paper measures something from text with a computer procedure, it should state the setting in which the texts are read: who produced them, for what audience, and what the language is taken as evidence about.
Why it mattersThe same passage can support several reasonable readings, so readers need the frame behind the measure.
CheckA passage setting out the frame the analysis works in: who produced the texts, for what audience, and what the analyst takes the language to be evidence about.
PassThe paper states the setting and standpoint from which its texts are read, in the text or in posted documentation.
FlagThe paper reports text-derived values with no passage stating the frame in which the texts were read.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-09severity if flagged: highmedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: supervised_text_classifiersilent: flag

A paper using a trained text classifier must report its agreement with human labels on documents withheld from fitting, broken out by category.

In plain wordsWhen the paper uses a trained classifier, a model that sorts documents into set categories, it should compare its labels with human labels on documents held out from training, reporting for each category both what share of its documents it found and how often it was right.
Why it mattersScores on training documents overstate accuracy, and an overall figure hides failures, because these two shares can differ.
CheckWhether the paper reports the classifier's agreement with human labels on documents held out of fitting, and whether that performance is broken out by category.
PassThe paper reports a comparison of machine labels against human labels on documents it states were not used in fitting or tuning the model, through a held-out set or a cross-validation procedure, and reports that comparison category by category: a cross-tabulation of human against machine labels, or per-category figures covering both the share of a category's documents the classifier found and the share of its assignments to that category that were right.
FlagNo comparison with human labels is reported; or figures are reported with no statement that they come from documents withheld from fitting, so a reader cannot tell whether the model was scored on its own training data; or only an overall accuracy or agreement figure is reported; or per-category figures are given for only one of the two directions of error. Each of these is a partial satisfaction and takes FLAG; the reviewer records which.

A paper using model-discovered categories must validate them, and anything derived from them, on its own corpus before substantive use.

In plain wordsWhen the paper uses categories or topics a model discovered in its texts, it should validate them on its own documents before drawing conclusions, for example by having people judge whether grouped documents belong together, and validate anything built from them too.
Why it mattersDiscovered categories need not match the concept of interest and partly reflect the model that produced them; success elsewhere does not carry over.
CheckWhether the paper reports a validation of its discovered categories, and of any quantity built from them, before drawing substantive conclusions.
PassThe paper reports at least one validation of the estimated categories performed on its own corpus, human judgement of whether documents within a category belong together and are separable from other categories, hand-coding a sample under the discovered scheme and checking agreement, or lining up movements in a category's measured attention against events known independently, and reports validation for any derived quantity it analyses.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-11severity if flagged: highlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: dictionary_measuresilent: flag

A paper scoring text with a word list must show the list fits this corpus or state it was built for this problem.

In plain wordsWhen the paper scores text with a word list (a dictionary of words tied to a concept), it should state that the list was built for this problem or show that a borrowed list works on these documents.
Why it mattersWords carry different meanings in different settings, so a list taken from elsewhere can badly mismeasure unless it is shown to transfer.
CheckWhether the paper demonstrates that its word list fits the corpus it is applied to, or reports building the list for this problem.
PassThe paper states that the word list was built for this corpus or research problem, or reports a demonstration that a borrowed list transfers to this domain, for instance checking list-based scores against hand-coded values for documents from this corpus.
FlagThe paper applies a word list taken from another domain or a general-purpose lexicon and reports neither a transfer demonstration nor a validation on its own documents.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-12severity if flagged: highlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: text_scalingsilent: flag

A paper scaling text must establish that the dimension it recovered is the one it claims to measure.

In plain wordsWhen the paper places texts or speakers on a scale using a model, it should show that the dimension recovered is the one it claims, for example by comparing placements with known positions or examining the words that drive the scale.
Why it mattersA scaling model latches onto whatever variation dominates the words, which may be topic, style, or tone rather than the intended dimension.
CheckWhether the paper shows that the dimension its scaling model recovered is the dimension it claims to have measured.
PassThe paper reports evidence about what the recovered dimension is, placements compared with independently known positions for the same actors, separation of groups known beforehand to sit at opposite ends, or examination of the words driving the dimension, and reports what that evidence showed.
FlagThe paper interprets the recovered dimension substantively with no such evidence reported, taking the model's output to be the intended dimension because the model was intended to recover it.

MEASUREMENT-IDENT-13severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: latent_variable_modelsilent: flag

A paper fitting a latent-variable measurement model must examine rival specifications and argue substantively for the one it keeps.

A paper aggregating expert ratings must model rater differences or defend the premises that averaging requires.

In plain wordsWhen the paper combines expert ratings, it should model rater differences in precision or scale use, test for such differences and tie its choice to the result, or defend the premise that raters are equally competent and read the scale alike.
Why it mattersA simple average assumes every expert is equally competent and reads the scale the same way; when that fails, the average misrepresents the quantity.
CheckHow the paper says it combined ratings across raters, and what it says about differences among them.
PassThe paper reports aggregating with a model carrying rater-specific parameters for precision, scale use, or category thresholds; or it reports a diagnostic bearing on whether raters differ in precision or scale use and relates its aggregation choice to that result.
FlagRatings are combined by a plain mean, median, or modal value with no reported diagnostic of rater differences and no statement defending the equal-competence and equal-scale-use premises.

A paper reporting cross-validated performance must state that all fitting steps were carried out inside the folds.

In plain wordsWhen the paper reports cross-validated performance (accuracy tested on rotating held-out portions of the data), it should state that every model-building step, including feature selection and tuning, was done separately within each training portion.
Why it mattersSteps done once on all labeled data leak information across the split, so the performance figure no longer means what it claims.
CheckWhether the paper states that its model-building steps were carried out within each fold.
PassThe paper states that feature selection, tuning, and any other fitting step were performed separately inside each training fold, or describes a pipeline from which that is explicit.
FlagThe paper reports cross-validated performance while describing feature selection, dictionary construction, or tuning as carried out once on the full labelled set, or says nothing about where those steps sat relative to the folds.

In plain wordsWhen the paper scores named cases, it should lay out the evidence behind each case's score, or state its coding criteria and apply them to at least two named example cases.
Why it mattersScores need to answer to what is known about each case, and readers cannot check reasoning they cannot see.
CheckWhether the paper sets out the evidence behind the scores it assigned to its cases.
