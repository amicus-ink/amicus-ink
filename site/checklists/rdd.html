Regression discontinuity

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

all
Every design the paper itself calls a regression-discontinuity (RD/RDD) design, i.e. it states that treatment is assigned by whether a running/forcing/assignment score crosses a fixed cutoff. Fires on the paper's own RD self-description; a paper that never claims an RD/threshold design is out of module.
covariate_adjusted
Fires only when the paper states it adds covariates to the RD estimating specification or otherwise conditions its point estimate on covariates. Near-miss that does NOT fire: reporting covariate balance purely as a diagnostic while the headline estimate is unadjusted.
fuzzy_rd
Fires only when the paper states its design is fuzzy / has imperfect compliance at the cutoff (the probability of treatment jumps by less than one, or it instruments received treatment with the cutoff indicator). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a sharp design the paper describes as perfect/deterministic compliance; using an instrumental variable elsewhere in the paper unrelated to the cutoff.
fuzzy
Fires only when the paper itself describes its design as fuzzy: it states that treatment take-up changes probabilistically (not deterministically) at the cutoff, or that it uses the cutoff/assignment indicator as an instrument for treatment actually received. NOT fired by: a sharp design that mentions occasional non-compliance in passing, or a paper that merely reports imperfect data coverage. If the paper never declares probabilistic take-up or an instrumenting step, the design is out of scope (NA).
pcrd
Fires only when the paper defines its RD treatment as a predetermined characteristic of the winning unit/candidate (a politician-characteristic RD), so the contrast is winners of differing types. Near-miss that does NOT fire: a standard party- or incumbency-status close-election RD where treatment is which party/incumbent won, not a candidate trait.
local_poly_datadriven
Fires only when the paper states it estimates the RD effect with local polynomial (local linear/quadratic) regression using a data-driven bandwidth it names (MSE-optimal, CER-optimal, cross-validated, or a plug-in selector). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a global polynomial fit over the full range with no bandwidth; a single ad hoc window the paper picks without naming a data-driven selector.
clustered
Fires only when the paper states its observation structure is grouped or repeated (multiple observations per unit, a panel of repeated events, or an explicit clustering/grouping level). Keys on the declared data structure, not on whether cluster-robust errors were used. NOT fired by: a single cross-section with one observation per unit. Ambiguous cases are NA.
discrete_rv
Fires only when the paper states its running variable is discrete or has few mass points (integer counts, coarse bins). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a continuous running variable rounded only for display; a continuous variable with many distinct values.
geographic_rd
Fires only when the paper states it uses a geographic/boundary RD with a spatial (two-dimensional) score or distance-to-a-border running variable. Near-miss that does NOT fire: a one-dimensional non-spatial score; a design that merely includes region fixed effects.
multiple_tests
Fires only when the paper reports a battery of several covariate-balance, placebo, or multiple-outcome tests (its own stated set of two or more such tests). Near-miss that does NOT fire: a single balance test or a single outcome.
heaped_rv
Fires only when the paper states its running variable heaps/bunches at particular (e.g. round) values. Near-miss that does NOT fire: a smooth continuous density with no reported heaping.

Identification10

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

RDD-IDENT-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must justify the RD design with a substantive argument that precise sorting at the cutoff is implausible.

In plain wordsThe paper should explain how the score that decides treatment (the running variable) is produced and argue why units could not precisely control which side of the cutoff they land on.
Why it mattersThe design is credible only if units could not place themselves on the side of the cutoff they preferred.
CheckWhether the paper gives a substantive account of how the running variable is generated and why precise sorting around the cutoff is implausible in this setting.
PassThe paper describes the score-generating mechanism and states a reason (institutional, timing, or informational) that units cannot finely control which side of the cutoff they land on.
FlagThe paper invokes the RD design but offers no substantive argument about the assignment mechanism or the possibility of precise sorting.

In plain wordsThe paper should control only for variables that were fixed or measured before treatment was assigned.
Why it mattersA control that the treatment could have changed can distort the estimated effect at the cutoff.
CheckFor each covariate entered into the estimating specification, whether the paper indicates it was determined prior to treatment.
PassEvery covariate the paper controls for is identified as predetermined (measured or fixed before treatment assignment).
FlagThe paper controls for a covariate that is realized after, or could be affected by, treatment.

RDD-IDENT-03severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must report at least one balance/falsification test (in a table or figure) showing a predetermined variable does not jump at the cutoff.

In plain wordsThe paper should report, in a table or figure, at least one test showing that a variable set before treatment, such as a baseline characteristic or an earlier value of the outcome, score, or treatment, does not jump at the cutoff.
Why it mattersIf the design is valid, variables the treatment could not have affected should show no jump at the cutoff.
CheckWhether the paper reports falsification tests for a discontinuity at the cutoff in one or more qualifying predetermined variables. Qualifying variables (sourced from the corpus): pre-treatment/baseline covariates, lagged values of the outcome, lagged values of the running variable, and lagged values of the treatment.
PassThe paper reports a test for a cutoff discontinuity in at least one qualifying predetermined variable and interprets the expected result as no discontinuity.
FlagThe paper reports no balance/placebo test on any predetermined covariate or pre-treatment outcome.
DisputedLee (2010) argues that in age/time-based RD designs, covariate-smoothness balance tests are generally uninformative because pre-threshold characteristics are identical by construction, so the required check does not help in that common design class.

RDD-IDENT-04severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper must rule out, or explicitly acknowledge, other treatments changing at the same cutoff.

In plain wordsThe paper should say whether any other policy or change switches at the same cutoff, and either argue the estimate reflects its treatment or define the effect as the combined bundle.
Why it mattersAnything else that changes at the same cutoff gets mixed into the estimate.

RDD-IDENT-05severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: covariate_adjustedsilent: pass

The paper must not claim covariate adjustment repairs a failed identification or balance assumption.

In plain wordsThe paper should present adding control variables as a way to gain precision or check robustness, not as a fix for a failed balance or design assumption.
Why it mattersAdding controls may sharpen the estimate, but it cannot repair a failed assumption that units just either side of the cutoff are comparable (balance or continuity).
CheckWhether the paper claims that adding covariates fixes detected imbalance or restores identification.
PassThe paper treats covariate adjustment as an efficiency or robustness step, not as a remedy for failed identification.
FlagThe paper reports imbalance or a continuity concern and asserts that covariate adjustment resolves it and restores a valid estimate.

A fuzzy-RD paper must invoke both the exclusion restriction and monotonicity to interpret its estimate causally.

In plain wordsIn a fuzzy design, the paper should state and argue two assumptions: crossing the cutoff affects the outcome only through treatment (exclusion restriction), and it moves treatment in only one direction (monotonicity).
Why it mattersA causal reading of the estimate requires both assumptions, so addressing only one is not enough.
CheckWhether the paper states the exclusion restriction (crossing the cutoff affects the outcome only through treatment) and monotonicity (no defiers).
PassThe paper states and argues both the exclusion restriction and monotonicity for its fuzzy design.
FlagThe paper gives a fuzzy-RD estimate a causal interpretation while stating neither exclusion nor monotonicity; state partial satisfaction as a FLAG when only one of the two is addressed.

In plain wordsThe paper should not limit its sample or define its outcome using something that happened after treatment, such as survival or running again, unless it shows treatment did not affect that variable.

Estimation1

Whether the estimator recovers the target parameter.

RDD-EST-01severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: pass

The paper's primary RD estimate must not rest on a global high-order (order 4+) polynomial.

In plain wordsThe paper's main estimate should come from a local fit near the cutoff or from a curve of order two or lower fit across the whole score range, not from a high-order polynomial (order four or more); a lone third-order curve is borderline.
Why it mattersSuch curves fit poorly at the cutoff and give estimates that shift with the chosen order and have unreliable confidence intervals.
CheckThe estimator behind the paper's headline RD estimate: whether it is a local (bandwidth-restricted) estimator or a global polynomial fit over the full range, and the polynomial order of that global fit.
PassThe headline estimate comes from a local estimator, or from a global polynomial of order two or lower.
FlagThe headline estimate comes from a global polynomial of order four or higher fit over the full running-variable range.
Thresholdcutoff 4; provenance: rule of thumb; borderline band: [3, 4]
Inside the bandOrder 3 (the border band) PASSes only if the paper also reports a local-estimator or lower-order specification giving the same conclusion; a lone order-3 global fit is a borderline FLAG. Order 4 and above FLAGs; order 2 or lower, or any local estimator, PASSes.

Inference4

Standard errors, tests, and their validity.

RDD-INFER-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: local_poly_datadrivensilent: flag

A paper using a data-driven MSE-optimal bandwidth must report robust bias-corrected (or undersmoothed) inference, not conventional CIs.

In plain wordsWhen the paper picks its bandwidth (the window around the cutoff) with a data-driven rule that balances bias and noise, it should report robust bias-corrected confidence intervals or use a narrower bandwidth, not conventional intervals.
Why it mattersThat data-driven bandwidth leaves bias in the estimate, so conventional confidence intervals at it are invalid.
CheckFor a paper using local polynomial estimation with a data-driven bandwidth, whether its confidence intervals are robust bias-corrected (or the bandwidth is undersmoothed below the MSE-optimal rate) rather than conventional intervals at the MSE-optimal bandwidth.
PassThe paper reports robust bias-corrected confidence intervals, or states it undersmooths the bandwidth for inference.
FlagThe paper forms conventional confidence intervals at a data-driven MSE-optimal bandwidth without bias correction or undersmoothing.
PrevalenceCalonico (2014a) notes the pairing of local-linear estimation with MSE-optimal bandwidths and conventional CIs plausibly affects many published RD studies.
DisputedHall (2015) advises reporting the more conservative of robust and conventional standard errors in small samples, endorsing conventional inference contrary to the requirement's exclusion of conventional CIs.

When interpreting a battery of balance/placebo/outcome tests, the paper should apply a multiple-comparisons correction or report a joint/omnibus test rather than reading individual rejections at face value.

In plain wordsWhen the paper interprets many balance, placebo, or outcome tests, it should correct for multiple comparisons or report a joint test, rather than reading each significant result on its own.

Reporting1

What the paper must state for a reader to evaluate it.

RDD-REPORT-01severity if flagged: mediummedium consensus, 2 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: flag

The paper should interpret its RD estimate as local to the cutoff, not as a population-wide effect.

In plain wordsThe paper should describe its estimate as the effect for units near the cutoff (and, in a fuzzy design, for units whose treatment responds to the cutoff), or name the assumption it uses to generalize.
Why it mattersThe design measures an effect at the cutoff, not an average across the whole population.
CheckWhether the paper states that its estimate is local to the cutoff (and, if fuzzy, applies to compliers) rather than presenting it as a general population effect.
PassThe paper describes its estimand as local to the cutoff, or names the additional assumption it invokes to generalize.
FlagThe paper presents its RD estimate as a population-wide/average effect without noting its locality or any extrapolation assumption.
If flagged, the author can fix it byState explicitly that the RD estimate is a local effect at the cutoff (for compliers, in a fuzzy design) and does not by itself identify a population-wide average effect; if you generalize beyond the cutoff, name the extra assumption you rely on.

Data1

Sample construction and measurement.

RDD-DATA-01severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: heaped_rvsilent: flag

A paper with a heaped running variable must run supplementary heaping-specific checks.

In plain wordsWhen many units share certain score values (heaping), the paper should run extra checks for heaping, such as dropping the heaped values or comparing heaped and other units.
Why it mattersWhen heaping is related to outcomes, standard checks can miss the resulting bias.
CheckFor a paper whose running variable heaps, whether it reports checks aimed at heaping-induced composition bias (e.g. disaggregated heaped-vs-non-heaped plots, a donut estimate dropping heap points, or covariate tests at heaps).
PassThe paper reports a heaping-specific diagnostic or remedy beyond the standard density/balance checks.
FlagThe paper's running variable heaps but it reports only standard diagnostics and no heaping-specific check.
