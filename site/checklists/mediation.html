The checklists

Mediation

12 checks, each resting on claims from the methods literature. The 10 sources listed at the end are the ones cited as further reading. Each check says when it applies, what passes, what is flagged, and where to read more.

How to read a verdict

Pass and flag are decided by the stated conditions. A check that is out of scope for the paper is marked does not apply; that is never a verdict on what the paper said. When a check applies and the paper says nothing, the verdict is the one given under silent.

A verdict covers only the text the audit could see. A flag on material the paper says is in an appendix or replication package means "not shown here", not "not done", and such flags carry that caveat in the report. Thresholds are rules of thumb unless marked otherwise, and near-threshold values are reported as borderline rather than rounded to a verdict.

When a check applies

A check applies only when its scope trigger fires. Triggers key on the paper's own explicit stance and default to out of scope when the paper is silent.

causal_mediation_claim
Fires when the paper describes one of its estimands with a mediation vocabulary term it applies to itself, 'indirect effect', 'direct effect', 'mediation', 'mediator', 'natural (in)direct effect', 'ACME', or 'mechanism/pathway' from a treatment through an intermediate variable to an outcome. Does NOT fire on a paper that reports only a treatment-outcome effect, only a correlation between two variables, or a moderation/interaction result, without naming an intermediate variable through which the effect is said to travel. The coder reads the paper's own labels; a paper that never calls any variable a mediator or any effect indirect is out of scope.
intermediate_confounder
The paper explicitly describes a variable that is affected by the treatment/exposure AND also confounds the mediator-outcome relationship (a post-treatment, intermediate, or exposure-induced confounder), OR states that it studies multiple mediators that causally influence one another. Fires only on the paper's own statement of such a structure. Does NOT fire when the paper adjusts only for pre-treatment baseline covariates, when it states its multiple mediators are causally unrelated, or when a coder merely suspects an intermediate confounder the paper never names. If it is unclear whether a described confounder is treatment-affected, the check is NA.
covariate_adjusted
The paper states it includes one or more control variables/covariates in its mediation model to address confounding, and names or lists them. Does NOT fire when the paper reports an unadjusted analysis using only exposure, mediator, and outcome, nor when a coder judges that covariates should have been added but the paper reports none.
nonlinear_binary_outcome
The paper states its outcome model is a nonlinear binary-response model (it names logit, probit, or another nonlinear probability model for a dichotomous outcome) AND reports a direct/indirect decomposition or a comparison of the exposure coefficient across models with and without the mediator. Does NOT fire when the outcome model is a linear regression or a linear probability model, nor when a logit/probit is fit for a purpose other than mediation decomposition.
coefficient_decomposition
Fires when the paper states it estimates the indirect (or direct) effect from regression/SEM path coefficients, a product-of-coefficients, difference-in-coefficients, Baron-Kenny, or path-analytic estimate. Does NOT fire on a fully nonparametric or simulation/imputation-based effect estimate that the paper does not describe as a coefficient product or difference. The coder keys on the estimator the paper names.
all
The paper reports a mediation analysis: it decomposes the effect of an exposure/treatment on an outcome into a part carried by at least one intermediate mediator variable, or reports an indirect / mediation / natural-indirect effect. Fires for any such analysis regardless of estimator. Does NOT fire for a plain regression whose covariates the paper never designates as mediators, nor for a moderation/interaction analysis with no mediator.
weighting_estimator
The paper states it estimates natural or interventional effects by an inverse-probability / weighting-based approach (weighting units by a function of the mediator or exposure density). Does NOT fire for imputation-, regression-, or simulation-based estimators, nor for the classic product-of-coefficients method.
large_scale_testing
The paper states it tests mediation for many mediators or units at once: a genome-wide or high-throughput mediation scan, many candidate mediators, many CpG sites, or the indirect effect evaluated across a grid of many time points. Fires on the paper's own declaration of multiplicity. Does NOT fire when the paper tests a single indirect effect or a small pre-specified set it does not treat as a multiple-testing family.
manipulation_induction
The paper states it uses an experimental manipulation designed to induce a particular psychological state or construct (e.g. an emotion or attitude induction) that serves as its treatment or the thing the mediator measures. Does NOT fire for observational mediators, nor for manipulations of non-psychological treatments where no induced-state verification is at issue.

Identification2

The assumptions the causal claim rests on.

MEDIATION-IDENT-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 8 sourcesapplies to: causal_mediation_claimsilent: flag

A paper claiming a causal indirect or direct effect must state the no-unmeasured-mediator-outcome-confounding (sequential ignorability) assumption its estimate requires; treatment randomization does not remove this obligation.

In plain wordsA paper that reads a direct or indirect effect as causal should state the assumption it relies on: that no unmeasured factor drives both the mediator (the in-between variable) and the outcome. Randomizing the treatment does not secure this assumption.
Why it mattersWithout this assumption, the estimated pathway is not a causal effect, and the assumption cannot be tested from the data.
CheckWhether the paper names the identifying assumption its mediation estimate relies on, that, given the measured covariates, no unobserved variable jointly drives the mediator and the outcome (equivalently, sequential ignorability / conditional independence of the mediator), rather than leaning on treatment randomization as if it settled that condition.
PassThe paper explicitly states the no-unmeasured-mediator-outcome-confounding (or sequential ignorability / conditional ignorability) assumption on which its causal reading of the indirect or direct effect depends, whether in the main text or any appendix or supplement.
FlagThe paper reports an indirect/direct/mediation effect and interprets it as a causal mechanism, but never states the no-unmeasured-confounding / ignorability assumption required for that interpretation, including a randomized-treatment paper that treats the mediation effect as identified on the strength of treatment randomization alone.
Further reading

  • Kosuke Imai, Luke Keele, & Teppei Yamamoto. (2010). Identification, Inference and Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Mediation Effects. Statistical Science, 25(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/10-sts321

MEDIATION-IDENT-02severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 6 sourcesapplies to: intermediate_confoundersilent: flag

A paper declaring a treatment-affected mediator-outcome confounder or causally-linked mediators must address the resulting non-identification of natural effects.

In plain wordsWhen a paper says a treatment-affected factor drives both mediator and outcome, or that its mediators affect one another, it should estimate an effect that stays recoverable, such as an interventional effect, or state that natural direct and indirect effects are not recoverable.
Why it mattersIn that setting, natural direct and indirect effects cannot be pinned down from the data, even in a randomized trial.
CheckWhether a paper that describes a treatment-affected mediator-outcome confounder or causally-linked mediators either estimates a still-identified quantity (e.g. interventional effects) or explicitly flags that natural effects are not identified here.
PassHaving declared an intermediate confounder or causally-ordered mediators, the paper targets an estimand that stays identified under that structure or states that natural direct/indirect effects are not identified.
FlagThe paper reports natural direct/indirect effects as identified despite declaring a treatment-affected mediator-outcome confounder or causally-linked mediators, without addressing the resulting non-identification.
Further reading

Estimation4

Whether the estimator recovers the target parameter.

MEDIATION-EST-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: covariate_adjustedsilent: flag

Each control covariate must be included in every equation of the mediation model.

In plain wordsEach control variable the paper uses should appear in both the model for the mediator and the model for the outcome.
Why it mattersA control left out of one of the models fails to remove the confounding it was added to address.
CheckWhether each declared control covariate appears in both the mediator model and the outcome model of the mediation system.
PassEvery control covariate the paper names enters all equations of the mediation model, including both the mediator and the outcome regressions.
FlagA named control covariate is entered in one equation of the mediation model but omitted from (or not reported for) another (e.g. in the outcome model but not the mediator model).
Further reading
  • Andreas Alfons, Nüfer Y. Ate s, & Patrick J. F. Groenen. (2022). Robust Mediation Analysis: The <i>R</i> Package <b>robmed</b>. Journal of Statistical Software, 103(13). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v103.i13, p. 6 (PDF pagination)
  • Richard Breen, Kristian Bernt Karlson, & Anders Holm. (2013). Total, Direct, and Indirect Effects in Logit and Probit Models. Sociological Methods & Research, 42(2), 164-191. https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124113494572, p. 174
  • Johan Steen, Tom Loeys, Beatrijs Moerkerke, & Stijn Vansteelandt. (2017). <b>medflex</b>: An <i>R</i> Package for Flexible Mediation Analysis using Natural Effect Models. Journal of Statistical Software, 76(11). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v076.i11, p. 35 (PDF pagination)
MEDIATION-EST-02severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: nonlinear_binary_outcomesilent: pass

In nonlinear binary-outcome models the paper must not read the indirect effect off a naive comparison of the exposure coefficient across nested models.

In plain wordsIn a logit or probit model of a yes/no outcome, the paper should not measure the indirect effect by how much the treatment coefficient changes when the mediator is added; it should use a method that keeps the scale fixed, such as a KHB-style decomposition or probability-scale effects.
Why it mattersIn these models, adding a variable rescales the coefficients, so that change mixes real mediation with rescaling.
CheckHow the paper obtains the direct/indirect split in its nonlinear binary-outcome model: a scale-invariant method versus a bare comparison of the exposure coefficient with and without the mediator.
PassThe paper derives the decomposition with a method that holds the scale and error fit constant (e.g. a single-model KHB-style decomposition, or effects expressed on the probability scale).
FlagThe paper infers the indirect effect from the change in the exposure's logit/probit coefficient between a model without and with the mediator, without holding scale and error fit constant.
Further reading
  • Richard Breen, Kristian Bernt Karlson, & Anders Holm. (2013). Total, Direct, and Indirect Effects in Logit and Probit Models. Sociological Methods & Research, 42(2), 164-191. https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124113494572, pp. 166, 170
  • Dustin Tingley, Teppei Yamamoto, K. Hirose, Luke Keele, & Kosuke Imai. (2014). <b>mediation</b>:<i>R</i>Package for Causal Mediation Analysis. Journal of Statistical Software, 59(5). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v059.i05, p. 5 (PDF pagination)
MEDIATION-EST-03severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 4 sourcesapplies to: coefficient_decompositionsilent: flag

A coefficient-product mediation estimate must allow for, test, or explicitly justify away a treatment-mediator interaction on the outcome.

In plain wordsA paper that computes the indirect effect by multiplying model coefficients should include, test for, or explicitly justify ruling out a treatment-by-mediator interaction, where the mediator's effect on the outcome differs by treatment.
Why it mattersMultiplied coefficients equal the natural indirect effect only when no such interaction exists.
CheckWhether a paper that estimates the indirect effect from a product/difference of coefficients allows for, tests, or explicitly assumes away a treatment-mediator interaction on the outcome.
PassThe paper includes a treatment-by-mediator interaction in the outcome model (and forms the indirect effect accordingly), tests for it, or explicitly states and justifies a no-interaction assumption.
FlagThe paper reports a coefficient-product indirect effect without including, testing, or justifying the absence of a treatment-mediator interaction.
Further reading
  • Kosuke Imai, Luke Keele, & Teppei Yamamoto. (2010). Identification, Inference and Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Mediation Effects. Statistical Science, 25(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/10-sts321
  • Zhonghua Liu, Jincheng Shen, Richard Barfield, Joel Schwartz, Andrea Baccarelli, & Xihong Lin. (2021). Large-Scale Hypothesis Testing for Causal Mediation Effects with Applications in Genome-wide Epigenetic Studies. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 117(537), 67-81. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2021.1914634, p. 68
MEDIATION-EST-04severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: causal_mediation_claimsilent: pass

A paper must not require a significant total treatment effect as a precondition for testing or claiming mediation.

In plain wordsThe paper should not require a statistically significant total treatment effect before testing or claiming mediation.
Why it mattersAn indirect effect can be nonzero even when the total effect is zero, so using the total effect as a gate is wrong.
CheckWhether the paper requires a significant total effect as a precondition before concluding or reporting mediation.
PassThe paper does not condition its mediation conclusion on first establishing a significant total effect (it estimates the indirect effect directly regardless of the total effect's significance).
FlagThe paper states that it tested (or required) a significant total treatment effect as a gate before proceeding to establish mediation.
Further reading
  • Kosuke Imai, Luke Keele, & Teppei Yamamoto. (2010). Identification, Inference and Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Mediation Effects. Statistical Science, 25(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/10-sts321

Inference3

Standard errors, tests, and their validity.

MEDIATION-INFER-01severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 6 sourcesapplies to: allsilent: pass

The paper must not rely solely on a normal-theory Sobel test for indirect-effect inference.

In plain wordsThe paper should not rely only on a Sobel test, which treats the indirect effect as normally distributed; it should use a bootstrap, a simulation-based interval, or a test built for the no-mediation null.
Why it mattersThe indirect effect is a product of two estimates, so it is not normally distributed and the Sobel test is invalid and weak.
CheckWhat method the paper uses to test or bound the indirect effect: a normal-theory Sobel z-test alone versus a bootstrap / Monte-Carlo / composite-null procedure.
PassThe paper's reported inference for the indirect effect uses a bootstrap, Monte-Carlo confidence interval, or a test constructed for the composite null.
FlagThe only inference the paper reports for the indirect effect is a Sobel / normal-approximation test calibrated against the standard normal distribution.
Further reading
  • Zhonghua Liu, Jincheng Shen, Richard Barfield, Joel Schwartz, Andrea Baccarelli, & Xihong Lin. (2021). Large-Scale Hypothesis Testing for Causal Mediation Effects with Applications in Genome-wide Epigenetic Studies. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 117(537), 67-81. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2021.1914634, pp. 73, 81
  • Andreas Alfons, Nüfer Y. Ate s, & Patrick J. F. Groenen. (2022). Robust Mediation Analysis: The <i>R</i> Package <b>robmed</b>. Journal of Statistical Software, 103(13). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v103.i13, p. 2 (PDF pagination)
MEDIATION-INFER-02severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: weighting_estimatorsilent: flag

A weighting-based mediation estimator must report standard errors that account for weight/mediator-model estimation uncertainty.

In plain wordsA paper that estimates mediation by weighting should report standard errors, such as bootstrap or robust ones, that account for the weights being estimated from a fitted mediator model.
Why it mattersDefault standard errors ignore this extra uncertainty and so are too small.
CheckWhether the paper's standard errors for the weighting estimator account for estimation of the weights, rather than using default model-based errors.
PassThe paper reports bootstrap or robust standard errors that incorporate the uncertainty from estimating the mediator model / weights.
FlagThe paper reports default model-based standard errors for a weighting estimator without accounting for weight-estimation uncertainty.
Further reading
  • Johan Steen, Tom Loeys, Beatrijs Moerkerke, & Stijn Vansteelandt. (2017). <b>medflex</b>: An <i>R</i> Package for Flexible Mediation Analysis using Natural Effect Models. Journal of Statistical Software, 76(11). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v076.i11, pp. 13-14 (PDF pagination)
MEDIATION-INFER-03severity if flagged: mediumhigh consensus, 3 sourcesapplies to: large_scale_testingsilent: flag

A paper testing many indirect effects must report a multiple-comparison correction.

In plain wordsA paper that tests many indirect effects should apply a correction for multiple comparisons across that set of tests.
Why it mattersReporting many tests without correction raises the chance of false positive findings.
CheckWhether a paper testing many indirect effects reports a multiple-comparison correction (family-wise or false-discovery-rate control) for that family.
PassThe paper reports a multiple-testing correction across its family of indirect-effect tests.
FlagThe paper reports significance for many indirect-effect tests with no multiple-comparison correction.
Further reading
  • Martin A. Lindquist. (2012). Functional Causal Mediation Analysis With an Application to Brain Connectivity. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 107(500), 1297-1309. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2012.695640, p. 1303
  • Michael B. Sohn & Hongzhe Li. (2019). Compositional mediation analysis for microbiome studies. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 13(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/18-aoas1210, p. 672

Robustness1

Sensitivity, diagnostics, and alternative specifications.

MEDIATION-ROBUST-01severity if flagged: highhigh consensus, 6 sourcesapplies to: causal_mediation_claimsilent: flag

A causal mediation analysis must report a sensitivity analysis for the untestable no-unmeasured-confounding assumption.

In plain wordsA paper reporting a causal direct or indirect effect should include a sensitivity analysis showing how its estimate would change if unmeasured factors drove both the mediator and the outcome.
Why it mattersThis assumption cannot be tested from the data, even under randomization, so the analysis shows how much the conclusion depends on it.
CheckWhether the paper reports a sensitivity analysis for the untestable identifying assumption, tracing the indirect/direct effect against a confounding parameter (residual correlation, coefficient-of-determination, or bound).
PassThe paper reports a sensitivity analysis for unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding (e.g. a sensitivity parameter, bounds, or a stated breakdown point), in the main text or an appendix.
FlagThe paper reports a causal indirect/direct effect but presents no sensitivity analysis for the untestable no-unmeasured-confounding assumption.
Further reading
  • Kosuke Imai, Luke Keele, & Teppei Yamamoto. (2010). Identification, Inference and Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Mediation Effects. Statistical Science, 25(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/10-sts321, p. 1
  • Dustin Tingley, Teppei Yamamoto, K. Hirose, Luke Keele, & Kosuke Imai. (2014). <b>mediation</b>:<i>R</i>Package for Causal Mediation Analysis. Journal of Statistical Software, 59(5). https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v059.i05, p. 36 (PDF pagination)

Reporting1

What the paper must state for a reader to evaluate it.

MEDIATION-REPORT-01severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: manipulation_inductionsilent: flag

A paper using an induced-construct manipulation must report a manipulation check.

In plain wordsWhen a paper induces a psychological state through an experimental manipulation, it should report a manipulation check, a measure showing the intended state was actually produced.
Why it mattersA manipulation rarely changes only the targeted state, so readers need evidence it produced the intended one.
CheckWhether the paper reports a manipulation check showing its induction produced the intended psychological state.
PassThe paper reports a manipulation check verifying the intended construct was induced.
FlagThe paper relies on an induced-construct manipulation but reports no manipulation check.
Further reading
  • C. Daniel Myers & Dustin Tingley. (2016). The Influence of Emotion on Trust. Political Analysis, 24(4), 492-500. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpw026, pp. 492, 496

Data1

Sample construction and measurement.

MEDIATION-DATA-01severity if flagged: mediumlow consensus, 1 sourceapplies to: causal_mediation_claimsilent: flag

A paper must establish that its mediator is measured after the treatment and before the outcome.

In plain wordsThe paper should show that its mediator is measured after the treatment and before the outcome.
Why it mattersA valid mediator is something that occurs after the treatment and before the outcome, so the order of measurement matters.
CheckWhether the paper establishes that its mediator is measured/realized after the treatment and before the outcome.
PassThe paper's design or measurement description places the mediator temporally after the treatment and before the outcome.
FlagThe mediator is measured contemporaneously with or before the treatment, or contemporaneously with or after the outcome, or the paper leaves the temporal ordering of treatment, mediator, and outcome unstated.
Further reading
  • Kosuke Imai, Luke Keele, & Teppei Yamamoto. (2010). Identification, Inference and Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Mediation Effects. Statistical Science, 25(1). https://doi.org/10.1214/10-sts321
